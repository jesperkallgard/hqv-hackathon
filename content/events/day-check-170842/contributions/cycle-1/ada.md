One internal site for what changed this week. A header with three ways in, a feed newest-first with an owner and date on every row, a footer that says who keeps it running.

- The home page is the feed, newest first — no dashboard, no charts, nothing to interpret.
- Every row names an owner and how often it updates. A row with neither doesn't earn its place.
- The header has exactly three ways in: the feed, who owns what, how we work. Nothing else.
- One orange pill on the whole site, for one action: post an update. Everything else is a navy outline.
- The footer names who keeps the site, plainly — not a credits list.

## Layout
- Header: site name "What's Changed" on the left, three nav items on the right — Feed, Who owns what, How we work.
- Content in one generous column below, white background, a lot of air, navy headings, text left-aligned.
- Footer: one line naming who keeps the site running (e.g. "Kept by the platform team").

## The feed (home page)
- A list of rows, newest first. Each row:
  - What changed (plain sentence, says what changed and what it meant — not what was done).
  - Owner's name.
  - How often it's updated (e.g. "weekly", "on release").
  - A small round tag for state — not a coloured word. States needed: shipped, testing, planned.
- Example rows to build with, so it reads real and not generic:
  - "Dealer locator now shows stock by store, not just by region." — Owner: Priya, Ops. Updated weekly. Tag: shipped.
  - "New checkout flow is being tested with 10% of traffic." — Owner: Sam, Payments. Updated on release. Tag: testing.
  - "Search is being rebuilt to handle typos." — Owner: Priya, Ops. Updated monthly. Tag: planned.
- No charts, no counts, no dashboard widgets. Just the list.

## Who owns what
- A page, not a modal: a plain list of areas (dealer locator, checkout, search, etc.) each with one owner's name. This is what the feed's owner names link to or come from.

## How we work
- A short plain-language page: what earns a post here (saves someone asking a colleague), how often things should be updated, who to ask if you don't know.

## The one primary action
- Not settled in conversation — assumed for this write-up: the single orange pill is "Post an update", available from the feed, opening a short form (what changed, owner, how often it updates). Group should confirm or change this next round.

## Tone
- Plain, concrete, short sentences. Says what changed and what it meant. No marketing language, ever.

## Out of scope
- No charts, no dashboard, no notifications, no comments.
