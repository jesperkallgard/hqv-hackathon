Every post has seven things on it. A list shows three — headline, owner, date. Click through and the other four are there: who it's for, the three-line summary, status, and one link onward.

- A post has exactly seven fields, always in this order when opened: headline, who it's for, a three-line summary, status or date, owner by name, how often it's updated, one link onward.
- A list row shows only three of the seven — headline, owner, date — so What's changed and the other areas stay one line per row; the rest waits behind a click, not in the row.
- The owner is a named person, not a team — this post is owned by Jonas B, not "Web team" — because a post that saves someone asking a colleague has to say which colleague.
- The first real post under this shape is 'My Husqvarna login moved', owned by Jonas B, updated monthly — proving the template holds for a real change, not a placeholder row.
- One post shape, everywhere. What's changed, What we're testing, How we do things and Who owns what all open to the same seven fields — there's one template, not four.

## What it is
The page a row opens into when you click its headline, in any of the four areas. Same shape every time:

1. **Headline** — My Husqvarna login moved
2. **Audience** — Anyone who signs in with a Husqvarna account
3. **Three-line summary** — "The My Husqvarna login moved from the old account portal on husqvarna.com to the group's single sign-on. Existing passwords still work; a password reset now comes from the new domain instead of the old one. Nothing on the storefront or checkout changed."
4. **Status / date** — Live since 12 Oct
5. **Owner** — Jonas B
6. **Updated** — monthly
7. **Link onward** — "Go to the new login page"

Layout: headline as the navy heading at the top, left-aligned. The other six fields follow it down the page in the order above, one after another, no tabs, no side panel. Flat panel, no shadow, white background — matches every other page on the site.

## Why the order
Headline and audience first, so a reader knows in two lines whether this is for them. Summary next, because that's the actual answer. Status, owner, cadence together at the end as the "who and when" block — this is deliberately the same three-piece block (owner, cadence, date/status) that already sits at the end of a What's changed row, just given more room. Link onward is last: it's the action, not the reason to read.

## The owner is a named person
This is a break from What's changed today, where rows are owned by "Web team" / "Content team" / "Conversion team". The facilitator's line this sprint — "owner by name" — means a real person on the post itself. We think this should replace the team name on the row too, not just live on the open page, but we didn't touch the existing rows; that's What's changed's page, not ours to edit here.

## What it refuses to do
No comments, no reactions, no "related posts", no second link. One link onward, because a post that offers three ways forward is a post that hasn't decided what it wants the reader to do next.

## Left open
Whether the audience field is free text or picked from a fixed list of teams/roles — we wrote it as a sentence here. And whether "Live since 12 Oct" is the right status wording for a change that's finished versus one that's ongoing, which the What we're testing area will need a different answer for.
