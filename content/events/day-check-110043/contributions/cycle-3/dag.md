What's Changed shows a line, an owner, a cadence and a date. The direction asks for seven things on every post. We built the missing three and used a real post to prove it: My Husqvarna login moved, owned by Jonas B.

- Every post carries exactly seven things: headline, audience, a three-line summary, a status or date, an owner by name, how often it's updated, and one link onward — no more, no fewer.
- A list row still shows only headline, owner and date. The other four fields exist but stay behind the click, not stacked into the row.
- The first real post is 'My Husqvarna login moved', owned by Jonas B, updated monthly — a name and a cadence someone can actually be held to, not 'Web team'.
- A post's date is when the thing changed, not when someone remembered to write it up. Cadence and date are allowed to disagree.
- This is the post shape for the whole site, not just What's Changed — whichever area gets built next uses the same seven fields, not its own version.

## What this adds

The canon's What's Changed page has rows with four things: a one-line description, an owner, a cadence ("meta"), and a date. The direction requires seven things on every post: headline, audience, a three-line summary, a status or date, an owner by name, a cadence, and one link onward. This closes that gap by defining the shape and writing one real post to it.

## The post shape (all seven fields)

1. **Headline** — short, plain, no verb missing: "My Husqvarna login moved."
2. **Audience** — who this is for, one line: "Customer service and store staff who get asked about My Husqvarna."
3. **Summary** — exactly three lines, short sentences, no marketing:
   - "My Husqvarna now signs in with email and password instead of the loyalty card number."
   - "Existing card-number logins still work until the end of the month."
   - "Password resets go through the email on file, not through customer service."
4. **Status/date** — when it changed, not when it was written up. For this post: "Live since 16 Oct." (This date is our placeholder — nobody confirmed it; swap for the real one.)
5. **Owner** — a name, not a team: "Jonas B."
6. **Cadence** — "Updated monthly."
7. **Link onward** — one link, to where someone would go next. For this post that's the My Husqvarna login help page, which doesn't exist yet — the link is `#` until it does. We are not inventing a destination for it.

## How it shows in a list vs. open

In the What's Changed list, a row shows only headline, owner, date — same as today, unchanged. The audience, summary, cadence and link onward only appear once the post is opened. This matches the direction directly: "In a list you see the headline, the owner and the date; the rest when you open it."

## What we did not touch

We didn't redesign the list itself, the front door, or build the other three areas (What we're testing, How we do things, Who owns what). This is the shape a post takes, wherever it lives, proven with one worked example on What's Changed. If another group is also touching What's Changed this sprint, this is meant to sit underneath their work, not replace it — the row format doesn't change, only what's behind the click.

## Left open

The real date this login change went live — 16 Oct is our placeholder. The real destination for the link onward — there's no login help page yet, so it points nowhere until one exists. Whether every existing example row on What's Changed gets upgraded to full seven-field posts, or just new ones going forward — we only built the one.
