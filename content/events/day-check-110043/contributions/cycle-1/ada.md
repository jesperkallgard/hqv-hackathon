One list, newest first, for the three things people keep asking colleagues instead of the site: what changed this week, what's being tested, who owns what.

- The first screen is one list titled "What's changed" — newest first, nothing else on it.
- It merges three kinds of update into one stream — site changes, live tests, and ownership — instead of three separate pages or tabs.
- Each row is one line saying what changed, plus an owner's name and a date. No description field, no categories shown, no search box.
- It refuses to say how the work happened — only what changed and what it meant, in the one line.
- A row only earns its place if it would have stopped someone asking a colleague — status noise and in-progress chatter stay off the list.

## Layout
One column, one list, titled "What's changed" as the page heading (navy, left-aligned). Below it, rows in a flat panel, no shadows, newest first. Nothing else above the fold — no search box, no filters, no tabs.

## Row format
Each row is exactly:
`<one line of what changed> — <owner's name>, <date>`

No second line, no description, no tags shown on the row.

## Example rows (written to stand in for real content — flag below)
- `Dealer locator moved to the new map component — Priya Nair, 14 Oct`
- `Homepage hero swapped for the autumn range — Tom Reyes, 9 Oct`
- `Checkout copy test now running on the basket page — Sara Lindqvist, 7 Oct`

These three example rows are ones we wrote ourselves, not real entries — the group asked us to answer the content question rather than supply it live. They stand in for the three recurring questions: what changed on the site, what's being tested, who owns the dealer locator. Whoever builds this should treat the wording and the owner names as placeholders, not as facts about real people or real changes.

## Note against the direction
The direction asks every post to name "how often it is updated" (a cadence). What we specified instead is a date (when it last changed). We kept the date because that's what answers "what changed this week" — a cadence answers a different question ("how often does this change"). If the room wants both, that's a gap for the next sprint, not something we invented an answer for here.

## Out of scope
No search box. No filters or categories on the row. No page per topic. No comments or replies on a row. No login gate — this is one shared list.

## Feel
Looks like something written, not designed: white background, a lot of air between rows, navy heading, flat panel with no shadow, left-aligned text throughout.
