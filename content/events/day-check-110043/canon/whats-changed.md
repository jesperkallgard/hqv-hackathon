## What the room decided

Two groups, Ada and Cleo, built the same page, and where they agreed the page does not argue: one column, one list called **What's changed**, newest first, nothing above it but the title. No search box, no filters, no tabs, no categories. Cleo's reason is the one the page holds to — if the list gets long enough to need a search box, the fix is to remove rows, not add search. One line per row, never two: a change that needs a paragraph is the wrong length, not a reason for a second line.

A row earns its place only if it would have stopped someone asking a colleague. Status noise and in-progress chatter stay off.

This page is now the first of four areas, reached from the front door. Nothing on the list changed.

## The disagreement, and which way it went

Ada specified a row as `what changed — owner, date`. Cleo specified `what changed — owner, how often it's updated`, because the direction asks every post to name its cadence. Ada argued for the date on the grounds that "what changed this week" is a question about when, and a cadence answers a different question.

The page carries both, and gives them different weight: the cadence sits with the owner where Cleo put it, and the date sits at the end of the row, quiet, because sorting newest-first is meaningless if the reader cannot see the dates it sorted by. This is the one place the merge added rather than chose, and it is the thing to argue about first.

## What we filled in

The room wrote three example rows each and said so. Both groups put Priya Nair's name on them; Cleo flagged that the name was not confirmed and Ada flagged the wording as placeholder. No invented change is hung on a named person here — the owner column names teams and roles instead, so nothing on the page claims something about a colleague that a reader cannot check. Real names go back in when the real owners are known.

Eight rows are shown rather than three, so the shape of a working list is visible. The wording of the first three follows the room's; the rest were written here. The line under the heading says so.

## Left open

The cadence-versus-date question above. Whether "updated whenever the store feed changes" is a cadence at all, or a way of saying there isn't one. How rows get onto the list, and who removes them when the list gets too long — the rule is Cleo's (trim, don't search), but nobody owns the trimming yet.
