## What the room decided

Dag and Bo built the same thing from two directions and agreed on the spine: a post is exactly seven things — headline, audience, a three-line summary, a status or date, an owner by name, how often it's updated, one link onward. No more, no fewer. A list row shows three of them; the other four wait behind the click rather than stacking into the row.

Bo set the order and the reason for it: headline and audience first, so a reader knows in two lines whether this is for them; the summary next, because that's the actual answer; status, owner and cadence together at the end as the who-and-when block, deliberately the same three-piece block that sits at the end of a What's changed row, given more room; the link onward last, because it's the action, not the reason to read.

The owner is a named person, not a team, because a post that saves someone asking a colleague has to say which colleague. Dag's point holds alongside it: a post's date is when the thing changed, not when someone remembered to write it up, so cadence and date are allowed to disagree.

This is the shape for the whole site. What's changed, What we're testing, How we do things and Who owns what all open to the same seven fields.

The post refuses the rest: no comments, no reactions, no related posts, no second link. A post that offers three ways forward is a post that hasn't decided what it wants the reader to do next.

## The disagreement, and which way it went

Both groups wrote the same post and wrote it differently. Dag had the login moving to email-and-password sign-in, live since 16 Oct, for customer service and store staff. Bo had it moving to the group's single sign-on, live since 12 Oct, for anyone who signs in with a Husqvarna account.

The page goes Dag's way, in full: Dag's audience, Dag's three lines, Dag's date. The reason is the audience — "customer service and store staff who get asked about My Husqvarna" names the people this post saves a question for, which is the test the site sets. Bo's version argued for the broader audience and the storefront reassurance ("nothing on the storefront or checkout changed"), which is worth keeping in the real copy if it is true. Both wrote Jonas B as owner and monthly as cadence, so neither is in doubt.

Bo's wording for the link onward — "Go to the new login page" — lost to Dag's refusal to invent a destination. The link points at `#` because the help page does not exist yet.

## What we filled in

Nothing on this page. The headline, audience, the three summary lines, the status, the owner, the cadence and the decision not to name a destination are all the room's, word for word. The link label names the page the room said it would point at once it exists.

## Left open

The date. 16 Oct is Dag's placeholder — nobody confirmed when the change went live; swap it for the real one. The destination for the link onward, which waits on a login help page being written. Whether the audience field is free text or picked from a fixed list of teams and roles — it is written as a sentence here. And whether "Live since" is the right status wording for a finished change versus an ongoing one, which What we're testing will need a different answer for.
