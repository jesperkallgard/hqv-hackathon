One page, one list, newest first: what changed on the site, what's being tested, and who owns the dealer locator. No sections, no search box — if you have to search it, it's already too long.

- One feed, not three pages — site changes, live tests, and dealer-locator ownership all land in the same list, newest first, under the title "What's changed".
- Each row is one line saying what changed and what it meant — not a log of tasks done.
- Every row names an owner and says how often that owner updates it — no row without a name attached.
- No search box, no filters, no tags. If the list is too long to scan, that's a sign something needs archiving, not a reason to add controls.
- This is the whole first screen. Nothing else competes with it for attention.

## What it is
A single page titled "What's changed". One column, one list, newest first. That's the whole screen.

## The row format
Each row is:
- One line: what changed and what it meant (not what task was done).
- The owner's name.
- The date it was last updated.

Example rows (format, not final copy — real owner names still need to come from the teams involved):
- "Dealer locator now shows stock by store, not just address. — Owner: [dealer locator owner], updated weekly."
- "Homepage hero swapped for the spring campaign. — Owner: [site owner], updated as needed."
- "Checkout A/B test running on the delivery step, ends the 30th. — Owner: [test owner], updated weekly."

## What it refuses to do
- No search box.
- No categories or tabs splitting the three kinds of update apart — they're one list on purpose, because that's how people actually ask about them ("what changed", "what's being tested", "who owns X").
- No comments, no reactions, no notifications.

## Look and feel
Matches the internal-site direction exactly: white, sparse, a lot of air. Navy heading. Left-aligned, one column. Flat rows, no cards, no shadows, no dividers trying to look designed — it should read like a page someone typed, not a dashboard.

## Open gap
The actual owner names for "site changes", "current tests", and "dealer locator" are not decided yet — that has to come from whoever runs those three things before this can go live with real content. The format above is settled; the names in it are placeholders until then.
