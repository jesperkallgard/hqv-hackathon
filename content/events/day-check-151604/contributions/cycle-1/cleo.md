An internal site called What's Changed opens on a Monday morning with a clear header, three ways in, and a feed that shows what changed this week, who owns it, and when it was last updated.

- The site name is What's Changed.
- The header has three ways in: feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The main view is a feed, newest first, with a person and a date on every row.
- A footer says who keeps the site.
- The look is calm and plain: white, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels, no shadows, and orange only for the one primary button.

It has to feel like a real internal website, not a document. It needs a header with the site's name and short navigation, one generous content column, and a footer. The page uses white space, navy headings, and left-aligned text. Panels are flat with no shadows. Buttons are fully round pills: orange with white text for the one primary action, outlined in navy for the rest. States are small round tags, not coloured words. The feed carries plain, concrete updates: what changed and what it meant, not what was done. Every post earns its place by saving somebody from asking a colleague, and every post names an owner and how often it is updated. The full shape is the header, the feed, the who owns what page, the how we work page, and the footer; no dashboard or charts.
