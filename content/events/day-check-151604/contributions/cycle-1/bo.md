A real internal site called What's Changed. It opens on Monday morning with a header, a short nav, and a single feed that gives the answer in one screen.

- The header shows the site name, What's Changed, and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The main content is a feed of what changed this week, what's being tested, and who owns the dealer locator, with the newest items first.
- Every row in the feed carries an owner and a date, so a post earns its place by saving somebody from asking a colleague.
- The site feels calm, white, and open, with navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels, no shadows, and orange used only for the one primary pill button.
- The footer says who keeps the site, and the page stays focused on speed: one screen, no scrolling, no charts, and no dashboard.

Build only the first-picture version of the internal site. It has a header with the site's name and a short navigation, one generous content column, and a footer. The navigation is the feed, who owns what, and how we work. The feed is the main answer screen and shows dated rows newest first. Each post must name an owner and how often it is updated. The look is white with a lot of air, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels, and no shadows anywhere. Buttons are fully round pills: orange with white text for the one primary action, outlined in navy for the rest. States are small round tags, not coloured words. No dashboard, no charts, and no other structure is in scope. The exact real example row content is still open.
