The first screen is four cards in one column: What’s Changed, What We’re Testing, How We Do Things, Who Owns What. Each card carries a last-update date, and ownership stays visible on the face of every card.

- An outsider can read the four card names and know where to click.
- The front door shows only four areas, no more.
- Each card is stamped with a last-update date.
- Every card names a person you can go and ask.
- The card face does not need more than the name, owner, cadence, and date; a one-line preview stays open.

Build a real internal website front door, not a document: white, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text, a header with the site’s name and short navigation, one generous content column, and a footer. The first screen contains exactly four cards in one column, with these names exactly: What’s Changed, What We’re Testing, How We Do Things, Who Owns What. Ownership is the main thing on the page, and every single thing on it names a person you can go and ask. Each card shows a last-update date. It is still open whether the card face shows only the name, owner, cadence, and date, or also a one-line preview of the latest item. Keep the rest of the site out of scope.
