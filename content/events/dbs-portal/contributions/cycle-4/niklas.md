The page keeps a dedicated Who to ask area, but it becomes a place where everyone can offer what they can help with, so people can find help without already knowing the right person.

- The Who to ask section stays on the page as a separate help area.
- Every person in that section contributes what they can help with, not just a name and title.
- The section answers the question “who can help with this?” in a way that is useful to someone outside the team.
- The content is about support and contribution, not about replacing the existing page structure.
- This change matters before anything else because it makes the page easier to use for people who arrive looking for help rather than for ownership.

Build a Who to ask section where each person states what they can help with. It has to feel like real internal website content, not a list of names: plain, scan-first, and understandable without DBS context. Keep the existing page structure; this is an added help section, not a new site. The section should carry real content about what each person can do, and it should be clear enough that a colleague can choose the right help without asking follow-up questions. If some people cannot yet contribute a useful description, that gap stays open rather than being filled with placeholder text.
