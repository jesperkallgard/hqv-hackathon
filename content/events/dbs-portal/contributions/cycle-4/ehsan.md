Make Kaj the first place a colleague goes for one vague internal question, so it answers what it can, points to the right owner or resource when it cannot, and makes the next step obvious fast.

- It sits in the existing page as a single change to the interaction model, not a new site or a broader internal search project.
- It handles vague questions about internal help and context by returning the right owner, next step, or resource instead of trying to answer everything.
- It says clearly what it can answer and what it refuses, so it does not overpromise on internal resources.
- It routes people elsewhere when the question needs another source, with the route shown as the immediate next step.
- It is the one thing to get right before anything broader, because the change is about being the front door rather than being an answer everywhere.

Build Kaj as the first-touch layer for internal help and context on the current site. The thing it must do is take one colleague question and either answer it from internal resources or send it to the right owner, next step, or resource. It must be narrow enough to avoid the rejected idea of being 'the answer everywhere'. It must state what it can answer, what it refuses, and where it routes people. The hand-in does not define the exact internal resource hierarchy, the full answer set, or the detailed routing rules; those remain open.
