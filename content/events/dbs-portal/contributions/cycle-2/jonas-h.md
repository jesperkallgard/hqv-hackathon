A start page for colleagues outside DBS who need the picture fast: what changed, what it means, and where to go next. It puts the most important cards first, lets people save the start page they care about, and keeps the route to previous show and tells and source in reach.

- The start page opens with a short introduction to DBS, then the main areas in a clear order.
- Each area card feels like the official site, with stronger visual hierarchy, profile pictures on people names, and one glance summary.
- People can favourite one start page or area as their main interest so the page can surface what matters most to them.
- Previous show and tell sessions sit on the page with summaries, source links, and the original material linked beside each item.
- A weekly activities card shows ongoing work and what is coming next, so the page works as a live front door rather than a static archive.

## What the page does
- Keep the page as a real internal website front door, not a wiki.
- Add an introduction section at the top that explains who DBS are and what they do, in plain language for colleagues outside DBS.
- Put the main area cards after the introduction.
- Make the cards closer to the official site look and feel: generous spacing, white background, navy headings, left-aligned text, one card per area.
- Add profile pictures next to people names wherever a person is shown as owner or presenter.
- Add a clear favourite action so a user can mark one start page or one area as their main interest.
- Add a section for previous show and tell sessions.
- Each show and tell item needs a short summary, a link to the source or original, and a link label that makes the destination obvious.
- Add a section for this week's activities.
- Add a list of ongoing activities and a list of upcoming activities.

## Content and structure
- Keep the current tone: plain language, no internal acronym in a headline, and say what changed and what it meant.
- Keep the page aimed at colleagues who do not work in DBS.
- The start page should make it obvious where the important content sits without needing a guide.
- The introduction should be short, not a long mission statement.
- The favourite control should be easy to spot but not louder than the primary page content.
- Previous show and tell entries should read like summaries of actual sessions, not placeholders.
- Ongoing and upcoming activities should be separate lists and should make the timing clear.

## Look and feel
- White, airy, and left-aligned.
- Use navy for headings and outlines.
- Keep buttons as fully round pills.
- Keep state labels as small round tags.
- Make the main content feel like one generous column with cards that can be scanned quickly.

## Out of scope
- No wiki-style page.
- No placeholder text.
- No new navigation system beyond the existing short nav.
- No extra dashboard or editing flow.
- No full redesign of the canon structure; this is a refinement of the start page only.

## Notes
- If a content field is missing, leave it visibly missing rather than inventing it.
- Keep all factual content real and specific.
- The page still has to read as a single start page, not as several separate pages.
