A named list of the content this site carries, so colleagues can see what belongs here, what each part is for, and who keeps it current.

- The site carries five named content buckets: updates, prototypes, polls, demo content, and operating status.
- Each bucket has a clear job: tell people what changed, let them try work, gather answers, support meetings, or show live issues.
- Every bucket names one person who keeps it up to date; owner means a person, not a team.
- Each item shows how often it changes, so people can tell what is static, monthly, or weekly before they open it.
- Explore and Optimise stay on the site as named areas, and the content list explains them in plain language for people who do not know the work.

## What this page has to do
- Present the content the DBS front door carries as a named list.
- Make each content kind easy to scan in one card-like block, in the existing web-page style.
- Keep the wording outcome-led: what changed and what it meant, not activity.
- Show who owns each content kind and how often it is updated.
- Keep the list limited to real content that the page can actually carry.

## The content buckets
- **What’s new** — blog-style release updates with images, video or animation where useful. Purpose: tell colleagues what changed and why it matters. Owner: the responsible developer or designer named on the post. Frequency: weekly or monthly.
- **Try this prototype** — links to prototypes people can test. Purpose: let colleagues experience a new flow or concept. Owner: UX designers. Frequency: weekly.
- **Polls** — short questions for people to answer. Purpose: collect quick feedback or a decision. Owner: the person running the topic, not a team. Frequency: as needed.
- **Demos and show and tell** — content for meetings and reviews. Purpose: let people catch up without attending live. Owner: the presenter or topic owner. Frequency: weekly or monthly.
- **Status and known issues** — current problems, blockers and what is being watched. Purpose: make risk visible. Owner: the named owner of the issue or area. Frequency: weekly.
- **KPIs** — graphs and year-on-year comparisons. Purpose: show results and direction of travel. Owner: analytics. Frequency: monthly.
- **Kaj** — the chat entry for quick questions. Purpose: let people ask for owners, next steps and context without digging. Owner: the DBS support/contact owner. Frequency: static.
- **Explore** — early concepts and experiments explained in plain language. Purpose: show what is being tried and why. Owner: the Explore lead. Frequency: weekly.
- **Optimise** — live experiment results and their meaning. Purpose: show what changed, what it meant, and what should happen next. Owner: the Optimise lead. Frequency: weekly.

## Copy and tone
- Write for colleagues who do not work in DBS.
- No acronyms in headlines unless the term is already common on the site.
- Use short, direct labels.
- Keep states as small round tags, not long status text.
- Keep one owner person per item.

## Out of scope
- No wiki-style documentation pages.
- No generic team ownership.
- No placeholder content.
- No second site structure; this is the front door list only.

## Assumptions
- The sprint was ended before the group could settle the final list, so this write-up uses the most sensible reading of the examples already given.
- Kaj is treated as a content bucket on the page because it was explicitly asked for as a direct input field, not just a link.
