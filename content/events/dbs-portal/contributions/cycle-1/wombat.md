The first screen is a plain list of what changed, why, and who owns it — built for two people: someone inside DBS who would otherwise Slack a colleague, and someone outside DBS who would otherwise book a meeting.

- The first screen is a list of posts, newest first — no landing page, no explainer, because both audiences arrive already knowing they need an update, not an introduction.
- Each row shows what changed and what it meant in one line, plus type, owner, and how often it's kept current — so a reader can judge trust before opening it.
- Filtering is by type and by team, not by search, because the two named needs are 'what's new in my area' and 'what's new full stop', not keyword lookup.
- Nothing on the first screen is dashboard chrome — no counts, no charts, no status pills with colour meaning something you'd need a meeting to explain.
- A post with no owner or no update cadence does not appear on the first screen at all, because the bar for being listed is the same as the bar for earning a place.

Assumption made because the sprint ended before this was settled: the two named audiences are (1) people inside DBS, who use the first screen instead of asking a colleague, and (2) people outside DBS — other teams, stakeholders — who use it instead of asking for a meeting. This is the most direct reading of the "earns a merge" bar in the brief and is the one thing this write-up assumes on the group's behalf.

**What the first screen shows**
- A single, one-column list of posts, most recent first. No hero, no summary cards, no metrics tiles.
- Each row (flat panel, no shadow, per the direction): title, one line of what changed and what it meant (not what was done), a tag for type — Playbook / Test result / Rollout status / Decision — the owner's name, and how often it's updated (e.g. "monthly", "on change").
- Two filters only: by type, and by team/area. No search box, no sort options beyond the default newest-first — the room agreed the need is narrow, not exploratory.
- Rows for posts without a named owner or update cadence are excluded from the list entirely, not shown greyed out or flagged — they simply are not eligible to appear.

**What it refuses to do**
- No personalised feed, no login-gated content, no "for you" section — both named audiences need the same list, not a curated one.
- No chart, count, or coloured status indicator anywhere on the first screen — anything that needs a legend belongs in a meeting, not here.
- No distinction in layout between the DBS reader and the outside reader — one screen, same content, because the bar is that an outsider can follow without help, which only holds if there isn't a separate "insider" view.

**Look and feel**
- Follows the direction as given: white, sparse, navy headings, flat panels, no shadows, one column, left-aligned text. Table-like rows with a navy header row and alternating light grey rows, the way the room's own decks look.
- Reads like a log that was kept, not a page that was designed.

**Open / out of scope**
- What exactly counts as "a team/area" for the filter (which named teams exist) was not settled this sprint — left open for whoever builds the filter list, rather than invented here.
- Whether a post can belong to more than one type was not discussed.
