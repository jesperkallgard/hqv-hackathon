A mid-market CFO wondering if consolidating their regional entities will wreck month-end close opens the page and sees their own close timeline next to what DBS makes possible — no pitch, just the calendar.

- The audience is named and specific: a mid-market CFO deciding whether to consolidate regional operations, not a generic visitor to a DBS overview page.
- Their real need is a decision input, not information: can DBS remove the three weeks of manual work multi-entity consolidation currently costs them.
- The first screen is a comparison, not a description — their current close timeline mapped against the DBS-enabled one, shown as calendar math.
- It is deliberately not a pitch: no feature list, no capability copy, no 'what DBS is' explainer on this screen.
- In ten seconds the takeaway is a magnitude, not a feeling: DBS cuts weeks, not hours, off close.

**What the first screen does**
1. Shows two timelines side by side (or stacked): "Your close, today" vs "Your close, with DBS" — both as calendar/week markers, not paragraphs.
2. The gap between them is the headline, expressed in weeks saved, not a percentage or a soft claim.
3. No call to action, no product tour, no "who to ask" links above this comparison — those belong deeper in the portal, not the first screen.

**What it refuses to do**
- Explain how multi-entity consolidation works underneath. That is a second screen, not this one.
- Sell. No adjectives about DBS's capability — the calendar does the arguing.

**Content we have**
- The specific cost being solved: multi-entity consolidation currently takes about three weeks of manual work at close.
- The claim we stand behind: DBS removes that — cuts weeks, not hours.
- The audience line, verbatim: "a mid-market CFO trying to figure out if they can consolidate their regional operations without blowing up their month-end close."

**What's still open**
We did not nail down the actual numbers behind the two timelines (how many days per entity, what the "with DBS" timeline looks like in real markers) — that's the next thing to pin down, not invented here.

**Note on direction**
No design or tone brief has been set yet for this hackathon. That matters here specifically because "not a pitch, just the calendar math" is a tone choice (plain, data-forward, no marketing voice) — whoever sets tone should know this group is explicitly arguing against pitch-style copy.
