A real internal site called What's Changed. It opens on Monday morning with a header, three ways in, a newest-first feed, and a footer. It feels calm, white, and navy, with one orange primary button and no dashboard or charts.

- The header shows the site name and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The main view is the feed: what changed this week, what's being tested, and who owns the dealer locator, with the newest items first.
- Every row in the feed names an owner and a date, so nobody has to ask a colleague who is responsible.
- The page uses white space, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels, and fully round pills for buttons and state tags.
- The footer says who keeps the site, and the site stays focused on ownership and updates rather than dashboards, charts, or marketing copy.

Build the first version of the whole internal site, not a document. It needs a header with the site name and short navigation, one generous content column, and a footer. The tone is plain and concrete, with short sentences that say what changed and what it meant. Every post must name an owner and how often it is updated. Use white space, navy headings, left alignment, flat panels with no shadows, and round pill buttons: orange with white text for the one primary action, outlined in navy for the rest. State labels are small round tags, not coloured words. Keep the scope to the feed, who owns what, and how we work; no dashboard, no charts, and no extra features beyond what the room has described.
