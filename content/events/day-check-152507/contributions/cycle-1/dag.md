An internal site called What's Changed opens on Monday morning with a calm white layout, navy headings, and one orange pill button. It gives people the feed first so they can get an answer in one screen without scrolling.

- The header shows the site name and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The main view is the feed: what changed this week, what's being tested, and who owns the dealer locator, with the newest items first.
- Each row in the feed shows an owner and a date, so the answer names who keeps it current and when it changed.
- The site includes a footer that says who keeps the site.
- There is no dashboard and no charts; the one open piece is the primary orange button, which is still unspoken in the sprint notes.

Build it as a real internal website, not a document. Use white space, left-aligned text, navy headings, and flat panels with no shadows. The site has a header with the name and short navigation, one generous content column, and a footer. Every button is a fully round pill: orange with white text for the primary action, outlined in navy for the rest. State labels are small round tags, not coloured words. Content is plain and concrete, with short sentences that say what changed and what it meant. Every post names an owner and how often it is updated. The site must fit on one screen without scrolling. The only thing left open is what the primary orange button does and who is allowed to use it.
