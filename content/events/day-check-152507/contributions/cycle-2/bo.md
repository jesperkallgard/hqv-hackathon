The front door is a single column of four cards: What’s changed, What we’re testing, How we do things, and Who owns what. An outsider reads those four and knows where to click.

- The first screen shows four areas and no more.
- The order stays fixed as What’s changed, What we’re testing, How we do things, then Who owns what.
- Each card shows the last update date.
- Every card names a person you can go and ask.
- The screen is enough on its own; there is no extra front-door content beyond the four cards.

Build a real internal website front door, not a document. Keep the header with the site’s name and short navigation, the generous single content column, and the footer. Use a white background, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text, and flat panels with no shadows. Make every button a fully round pill: orange with white text for the one primary action, outlined in navy for the rest. States are small round tags, not coloured words. On the first screen only, show four cards in one fixed order: What’s changed, What we’re testing, How we do things, Who owns what. Put the last update date on each card and an owner name on each card. The front door does not sort cards by recent updates, and it does not add any other sections or extra home content.
