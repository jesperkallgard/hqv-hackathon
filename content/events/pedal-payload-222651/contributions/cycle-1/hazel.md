We started from what actually stops someone mid-scroll wondering "does this fit me and my budget" — and cut everything else.

The page shows exactly five things, in this order:
1. A big, clear photo of the bike — the thing people actually want to look at first.
2. The price, right there, no digging.
3. Frame size — S/M/L/XL as simple pills you tap, so you can picture yourself on it.
4. Weight, stated plainly, because it's the one number people quietly worry about and rarely find.
5. Three honest bullets on what makes it ride different — written in what you'll feel, not spec-sheet language. No "aluminum alloy frame," instead "wider tires soak up potholes, you'll feel fewer jolts."

We left out reviews, a color picker, and any upsell on purpose. None of those answer "does this work for me" — they're there to keep you on the page, not to help you decide.
