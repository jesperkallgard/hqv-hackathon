A page format for the internal site: it answers one specific question someone would otherwise ask a colleague, and it says who owns the answer and how often it's checked.

- Every page answers exactly one question someone would otherwise message a colleague to ask.
- Every page names one owner by name, not a team.
- Every page states how often it's updated — weekly, monthly, or on change — right under the title.
- No page is a general update or a status report. If it doesn't answer a question, it doesn't go up.
- Plain text, no summary paragraph at the top repeating the title — the page starts with the answer, not with context.

## What it is
A single page format, used across the site: one question, one answer, one owner, one update cadence.

Structure of a page, top to bottom:
1. Title, written as the question someone actually asks (e.g. "Who owns the deploy pipeline?")
2. Owner — a name, e.g. "Owner: Bo"
3. Update cadence — e.g. "Updated: monthly" or "Updated: when it changes"
4. The answer, in short sentences, saying what changed and what it meant — not a process writeup.

## Assumption made (not yet confirmed by the group)
Dag asked to hand in before we picked a real example question. We used "Who owns the deploy pipeline?" / Owner: Bo / Updated: monthly as a placeholder to show the shape of the format. This is not a decided example — the group should replace it with their own real question before it's treated as content.

## Look and feel
Follows the day's direction: white, sparse, a lot of air, navy headings, left-aligned, one column, flat panels, no shadows. Looks written, not designed.

## Out of scope
Not a wiki. Not a place for status updates, announcements, or anything without a named owner. No comments, no search, no navigation beyond a plain list of pages for this sprint.
