A calm internal site called What's Changed. It opens on a Monday with a header, three short ways in, and the feed in one generous column. Every post names an owner and a date so nobody has to ask a colleague.

- The header shows the site name and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The main view is a single feed called What's Changed, with newest items first.
- Every row in the feed has a change, an owner's name, and a date.
- The site includes a who owns what area and a how we work area, plus a footer that says who keeps the site.
- The look is white, spacious, left-aligned, with navy headings, flat panels, no shadows, orange for the one primary pill button, and outlined navy pills for the rest.

Build a real internal website, not a document. Keep it to a header, one generous content column, and a footer. The site name is What's Changed. The top navigation has exactly three entries: the feed, who owns what, and how we work. The feed is the main thing users see first on Monday morning. It is a list of what changed this week, what's being tested, and who owns the dealer locator, with newest first. Every item must name an owner and a date, because worth keeping means it saves somebody from asking a colleague. Include the who owns what section and the how we work section, but do not add charts or a dashboard. Keep the tone plain and concrete, with short sentences. Make it white and airy, with navy headings and left-aligned text. Panels are flat with no shadows. Buttons are fully round pills: orange with white text for the one primary action, outlined in navy for the rest. States are small round tags, not coloured words. The footer says who keeps the site.
