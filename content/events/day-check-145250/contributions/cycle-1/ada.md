A real internal site called What's Changed. It opens with a header, short navigation, and a single feed of owned updates so someone can get an answer in one screen on Monday morning.

- The site uses a header with the name and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The main content is one generous column with the feed first: what changed this week, what's being tested, and who owns the dealer locator, newest first.
- Every row shows an owner, a date, and a small round state tag such as shipped, being tested, or planned.
- The page is calm and plain: white background, a lot of air, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels with no shadows, and orange only for the one primary pill button.
- A footer says who keeps the site, and the whole thing stays focused on speed so somebody can get an answer without scrolling; no dashboard and no charts.

Build a real internal website, not a document. Use a header with the site's name and short navigation. Put the content in one generous column with a footer. Keep it white, airy, and left-aligned, with navy headings and flat panels with no shadows. Use fully round pill buttons: one primary orange button with white text, and the rest outlined in navy. State is shown with small round tags, not colored words. The site content is the feed, who owns what, and how we work. The feed holds dated rows that answer what changed, what's being tested, and who owns the dealer locator. Every post must name an owner and how often it is updated because a post only earns its place if it saves somebody from asking a colleague. The goal is that somebody can get their answer in one screen without scrolling. No dashboard, no charts.
