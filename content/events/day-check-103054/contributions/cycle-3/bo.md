Every row in "What's changed" becomes a post. The list still shows just headline, owner, date — open one and you get four more things: who it's for, three lines of summary, its status, and one link onward.

- Every row in What's changed becomes a post you can open — the feed grows a click-through, it isn't a separate content type.
- The list view is unchanged: headline, owner, date. Nothing new is added to the row itself.
- Opening a post reveals four more things: audience, a three-line summary, a status or date, one link onward.
- Audience is a name, not a segment guess — who the post is actually for, said plainly.
- One link onward, never more. A post points to one place to act or read further, not a menu of links.

## What it is
The rows already built in "What's changed" become posts. The feed itself doesn't change shape — headline, owner, date, newest first, no sections. Click a row and it opens to a post with seven things on it total: the three from the list, plus audience, a three-line summary, a status or date, and one link onward.

## Example post (real content, first one written)

- **Headline:** My Husqvarna login moved
- **Audience:** Anyone who logs into myhusqvarna.com
- **Summary (three lines):** Login moved from the old portal to husqvarna.com/login on 10 March. Old bookmarks redirect automatically for 90 days. Password reset works the same way it did before.
- **Status / date:** Live since 10 March
- **Owner:** Jonas B
- **Updated:** monthly
- **Link onward:** the login page itself, husqvarna.com/login

The headline, owner, and cadence were given to us directly. The audience, summary, status wording, and the link were written by us to show the shape of a real post — swap in the real ones when this is built.

## Look and feel
Same direction as the rest of the site: white, sparse, navy heading for the headline, one column, left-aligned, flat panel, no shadow. The post reads top to bottom in a fixed order: headline, then the three-line summary, then status, owner, cadence, then the one link onward at the bottom. No sidebar, no related posts, no tags.

## Out of scope
- No comments on a post.
- No related-posts list or "see also".
- No more than one link onward — if a post needs three links, that's three posts.
- No rich formatting in the summary — three lines of plain text, not a body with headings and images.
