The internal homepage is one list, titled "What's changed," newest first — so nobody has to message a colleague to find out what shipped, what's being tested, or who owns the dealer locator.

- One list, titled "What's changed," is the whole first screen — no dashboard, no tiles, no sections to choose between.
- Newest first, and that is the only ordering there is. No pinning, no priority.
- Each row is one line of text, an owner's name, and a date. Nothing else sits on the row.
- It replaces three separate questions people keep asking a colleague: what changed on the site this week, what is being tested right now, and who owns the dealer locator.
- There is no search box. You read down the list; you do not search it.

## What it is
A single page, one column, titled "What's changed." Below the title, a list of rows, newest first. That is the entire page — no filters, no tabs, no categories, no search box.

## A row
One line of plain text describing what changed and what it meant, then an owner's name, then a date. Example rows (wording is our own, to show the shape — the facilitator's real content can replace these):

- "Dealer locator now points at the new map provider. Old links stopped resolving for two days." — Dag — 12 March
- "Checkout copy test is running on half of visitors this week." — Ada — 10 March
- "Dealer locator is owned by Dag. He updates this line whenever ownership changes." — Dag — 1 March

The three rows above are our stand-in for the three questions the facilitator said people keep asking: what changed on the site, what's being tested, and who owns the dealer locator. An ownership row (like the third) isn't a "change" in the usual sense — it's a fact that gets restated whenever it's confirmed or handed over, so the date tells you how current it is.

## Owner and date, always
Every row names one person, never a team, and a date. No row without both. The date is when that line was last true, not when the row was created.

## Out of scope
- No search box.
- No filters, tags, or categories.
- No comments or reactions.
- No pagination mentioned yet — assume the list just keeps going and gets long; that's fine, it's meant to be scanned, not browsed.

## Look
White background, a lot of air between rows. Navy heading for "What's changed." Left-aligned, one column, no shadows, no cards with borders — flat. It should read like someone typed it, not like it was designed.

## Assumption
The three example rows' exact wording is ours, not the facilitator's — we invented plausible text to show the shape of a row. The title "What's changed," the row format (line + owner + date), and no search box are the group's actual decision, not an assumption.
