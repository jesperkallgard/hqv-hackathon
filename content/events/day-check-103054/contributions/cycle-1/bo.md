One feed, newest first, that answers the three things people keep asking a colleague instead: what changed, what's being tested, who owns the dealer locator.

- One list, newest first — not three pages for changes, tests, and ownership.
- Every row is one line, an owner's name, and a date. Nothing else on the row.
- No search box, no filters, no categories. If it needs to be searched, it doesn't belong in this feed.
- It's titled 'What's changed', not 'Updates' or 'Activity' — plain, not a product name.
- It only earns its place if it stops the three questions we get asked every week: what changed on the site, what's being tested, who owns the dealer locator.

## What it is
A single list on one screen. Newest entry at the top. No sections, no tabs, no separate pages for the three kinds of thing that go in it — a site change, a live test, and a piece of ownership all sit in the same feed, in the order they happened.

## The row
One line of text, an owner's name, and a date. That's it. No description field, no tags, no status badges, no thumbnails.

Example rows (real content, not placeholders — assumed for this hand-in since we didn't have live examples on hand, see note below):

- Homepage hero image changed to the spring line — Priya, updated weekly
- A/B test live on the checkout button colour since Monday — Tomas, checked daily
- Dealer locator owned by the Facilities team — Jonas, reviewed monthly

## Look and feel
Follows the day's direction exactly: white, sparse, a lot of air. One column, left-aligned. Navy heading "What's changed" at the top, nothing else near it — no subtitle, no icon, no counter of how many items. Flat panel per row, no shadow, no card styling that makes it look like a product feed. It should read like a page someone typed, not a dashboard someone designed.

## Out of scope
- No search box.
- No filters or categories.
- No comments or replies on a row.
- No notifications or email digest — this is a page you go to, not one that comes to you.

## Note
We answered the "what would a real row look like" question ourselves rather than pulling it from the group, since we didn't have this sprint's live data in front of us. The three example rows above are the assumption — the actual site-change, test, and ownership entries should be swapped in for real ones when this gets built.
