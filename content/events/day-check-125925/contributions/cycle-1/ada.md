A page of short posts about what changed on the team and what it meant — not a wiki, not a wall. Each post has an owner and says how often it's kept current.

- The page is a list of posts, newest first, one column, nothing else on it.
- Every post names an owner by name — not a team, a person you can go and ask.
- Every post states how often it's updated, in words a person wrote, not a system timestamp.
- A post says what changed and what it meant, never what was done or who did the work.
- Nothing goes on the page unless it would otherwise mean someone asking a colleague — it refuses status updates nobody would ask about.

## Assumption
Ada handed this in without specifying the exact content, so this was written from the direction alone: "something for the team, a page, probably." The shape chosen is a team log — a page of dated, owned posts about internal changes. This can be corrected in a later hand-in if it's wrong.

## What's on the page
- A single column list of posts, most recent at the top.
- Each post has: a plain heading stating what changed, one or two short sentences on what it meant (not what was done), an owner's name, and how often that post is kept current (e.g. "updated when it changes", "checked every sprint").
- No categories, no tags, no search, no comments. Just the list.

## Look and feel
Follows the direction as given: white, sparse, a lot of air. Navy headings. Left-aligned, one column. Flat panels, no shadows. It should read like something someone wrote, not something someone designed — no cards-with-icons, no marketing layout.

## Tone
Plain and concrete. Short sentences. States the change and its consequence. No "we're excited to announce," no marketing language anywhere.

## Out of scope
- No editing UI, no CMS — the content is static for now.
- No notifications, no subscriptions.
- No author avatars or profile pictures — a name is enough.

## Open
The actual first posts (real content) were not supplied this sprint — Ada and Dag didn't have time to write real examples. Whoever builds this should use clearly placeholder-but-plausible entries and flag that real content is needed, rather than inventing facts about the team.
