One page for the team: short posts on what changed and what it meant, each with an owner and how often it's kept up. Built to replace the message you'd send a colleague, not to be read like a newsletter.

- One column, one list, newest post first — not a wiki, not a dashboard, not a set of tabs.
- Each post says what changed and what it meant, never a log of what was done.
- Every post names an owner and how often it's updated, so you know who to ask and whether it's stale.
- Nothing gets posted unless it would otherwise be a question to a colleague — status-for-its-own-sake doesn't earn a place.
- No comments, no reactions, no marketing language. It's read, not engaged with.

## What's on the page
A single left-aligned column of posts, most recent first. Each post is short: a heading, one or two lines saying what changed and what it meant for the team, an owner name, and an update cadence line ("updated weekly", "updated when it changes").

## Not decided yet — open
The group didn't settle on real example content this sprint. Placeholder posts below are illustrative only and should not be treated as real:

- "Deploy pipeline moved to the new runner — owner: Bo — updated weekly"
- "API rate limits changed for internal callers — owner: Cleo — updated when it changes"

The real first posts (actual topics, actual owners) are still open and should come from the team in a later sprint.

## Look and feel
Follows the day's direction: white, sparse, a lot of air. Navy headings. Flat panels, no shadows, no cards-with-borders styling. Left-aligned, one column throughout. Should read like something typed and posted, not laid out.

## Out of scope
No comment threads, no reactions, no notifications, no search — just the list. No dashboard-style metrics or charts.
