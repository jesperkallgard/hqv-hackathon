Every post is the same shape wherever it appears. The list shows three fields; opening the post shows the other four. First worked example: "My Husqvarna login moved."

- A post is always the same seven fields — headline, audience, three-line summary, status or date, owner by name, update cadence, one link onward. Not six, not eight, no area gets its own extra field.
- A list row shows exactly three of the seven: headline, owner, date. The rest only exist once you open the post — that is the one click the reader does.
- The worked example is real, not a placeholder: 'My Husqvarna login moved', owned by Jonas B, updated monthly — proving the shape holds for an IT-style change, not only a product one.
- The three-line summary says what changed and what it means for the reader, never what the team did — 'you'll be asked to sign in again once', not 'we migrated the login service'.
- One link onward means one link. It goes to the single place that answers the obvious next question, and a post that wants two links onward is actually two posts.

## The post, opened
Flat panel, no shadow, navy heading, left-aligned, one column. Fields in this order:

- **Headline:** My Husqvarna login moved
- **Audience:** Anyone who signs into myhusqvarna.com to manage a dealer account
- **Summary (three lines):** The login page moved behind single sign-on. You'll be asked to sign in again the first time after 10 March. Nothing else about the account changes.
- **Status:** Live since 10 March
- **Owner:** Jonas B
- **Updated:** Monthly
- **Link onward:** Go to the new login page

## The post, in a list
Just headline, owner, date: "My Husqvarna login moved — Jonas B — 10 March." The other four fields are not summarised or teased in the list; they only exist behind the open.

## Where it sits
Assumed this post belongs under "What's changed", since it describes something that happened rather than something being tested or an ownership note. Not decided by this group: whether the other three doors (What we're testing, How we do things, Who owns what) use this exact same seven-field shape or a variant of it — that's for whoever builds those.

## Out of scope, on purpose
No second link. No extra fields per area. What happens to a post once its status goes stale is not decided here.

## Assumption made
Only the headline, owner and cadence were given. The audience, three-line summary, status wording and link onward are filled in to make the example real and judgeable — correct them and we hand in again.
