The homepage is four cards, one column, one per area. An outsider reads the four names and knows where to click before they've clicked anything.

- The homepage is four flat cards stacked in one column — not the changed-list itself, not a dashboard, not a nav bar with a dropdown.
- The four cards are named exactly, in this order: What's changed, What we're testing, How we do things, Who owns what. No fifth card, ever, and no renaming any of the four.
- Each card carries only its name and the date its area last changed — no summary line, no count of items, no preview of what's inside.
- A card's date belongs to the area behind it, not to the front door. If an area hasn't moved in months, its card says so by staying quiet while the others move.
- Sprint 1's list is not repeated or summarised on the front door. It becomes the page you land on when you click 'What's changed', unchanged.

## What it is
The front door replaces the single-list homepage from sprint 1 with four cards in one column. Clicking a card goes to that area's own page. The front door itself holds no content beyond the four cards — no intro paragraph, no search, no "recently updated" strip.

## The four cards, exact names and order
1. What's changed
2. What we're testing
3. How we do things
4. Who owns what

These are fixed. Nobody adds a fifth or splits one into two without going back to the room.

## What's on a card
Just the name and a date: "What's changed — updated 14 March." Flat panel, no shadow, navy heading, left-aligned, generous white space between cards — same visual language as sprint 1's list rows, just fewer words per row.

## Whose date is whose
The date on a card is the last time that area's own page changed, not when the front door was built. "What's changed" already has content from sprint 1 (dealer locator hours, the checkout test, dealer locator ownership) — that card's date is 14 March, the most recent of those rows.

## Out of scope, on purpose
- What lives behind "What we're testing," "How we do things," and "Who owns what" is not this group's call — that's for whichever group takes each of those areas.
- No icons, no card counts, no colour-coding by "how recently updated."
- What a card looks like with an area that has never been updated is not decided — left open rather than invented.

## Assumption made
No brief was recorded splitting this sprint's areas among the groups, so Team Bo took the front door itself as its part, since that's what the group had settled on discussing. If another group also built the front door, that collision gets sorted in the vote.
