One list, newest first, nothing else on the first screen. It answers what changed, what's being tested, and who owns what — in the same three lines you'd get from asking a colleague.

- One list titled "What's changed", newest first. That is the whole first screen — no sections, no tabs, no other content above or below it.
- Every row is one line, plus an owner's name and a date. Nothing else on the row.
- The three questions we keep getting asked — what changed, what's tested, who owns it — are answered by the same kind of row, in plain language, not sorted by type.
- No search box and no filters. If the list gets too long to scan, that's a sign to prune old rows, not to add search.
- A row only earns its place if it would otherwise mean someone asking a colleague — which is why an owner's name and how often it's updated are not optional, on any row.

## What it is
A single page, one column, titled "What's changed". Below the title, a plain list of rows, newest first. Nothing above the list except the title. Nothing below it except more rows.

## A row
One line of plain text, then an owner's name, then a date. No icons, no tags, no category label, no colour-coding by type. Example rows, in the group's own wording:

- "Dealer locator now shows a store's opening hours instead of just its address. — Bo, 14 March"
- "Testing a new checkout flow on 10% of traffic. — Ada, 12 March"
- "Dealer locator is owned by Bo. Updated monthly." — this is how an ownership question gets answered: as a row like any other, not a separate "who owns what" table.

A change, a test, and an ownership note all look the same on the page — one line, one owner, one date. The reader works out which kind it is from the sentence, the same way they would if a colleague told them.

## What it refuses to do
- No search box, no filter, no sort control. The list is the whole feature.
- No categories or tags splitting changes from tests from ownership notes — that would turn one list back into three.
- No comments, no reactions, nothing that turns this into a discussion.

## Look and feel
Follows the day's direction directly: white background, a lot of air, navy heading, left-aligned, one column, flat rows with no shadow or card styling — it should look like a written note, not a designed page. Rows are separated by plain space or a thin line, not boxes.

## Left open
How far back the list goes, and whether old rows ever roll off, was not decided. That's a gap for whoever picks this up next, not a guess we're making now.
