One list, newest first, for the three things people keep asking a colleague instead of checking: what changed this week, what's being tested, who owns the dealer locator.

- One list, newest first, and nothing else — no tabs, no filters, no search box.
- Every row is one line of what happened, an owner's name, and a date. No bodies, no summaries, no explanations of why.
- A row only goes up if it answers one of three questions: what changed on the site this week, what's being tested right now, or who owns something people keep asking about.
- No row without a named owner gets posted — an update with nobody's name on it is the thing this page exists to stop.
- It's called 'What's changed', not a dashboard, because it should read like a log someone kept, not a page someone designed.

## What it is
A single page, one column, one list. Newest entry at the top. No categories, no tabs, no search box, no pagination control — if the list gets long, that's a later problem, not this one.

## Row format
Each row: one line of plain text, then an owner's name, then a date. Nothing else on the row. Example rows, in the real voice this should read in:

- Dealer locator moved to the new store-finder API. — Priya Shah, 3 Mar
- Home page hero swapped to the March push. — Tom Reyes, 1 Mar
- Checkout button colour test live: navy vs grey. — Marcus Lee, 28 Feb
- Dealer locator — owned by Priya Shah, updated whenever a dealer opens or closes. — 3 Mar

That fourth row is the answer to "who owns the dealer locator": a standing row, not tied to a change, that just states the owner and how often it's touched. It sits in the same list, sorted by its date like everything else, not pinned or separated out.

## Look and feel
White page, a lot of air, one column, text left-aligned. Navy for the page title only ("What's changed") — rows are plain black text on white, flat, no panels with shadows, no cards. It should look typed, not laid out: like someone kept a running note, not like a status dashboard with widgets.

## What it refuses to do
- No search box.
- No tabs or filter chips for the three types of question — they live in one list together, not sorted into sections.
- No body copy under a row, no "read more".
- No row posted without an owner's name attached.

## Left open
How far back the list goes, and what happens once it's long (pagination, an archive) — not decided, out of scope for this.
