A page of short posts that replace the questions people currently walk over and ask a colleague. Each one names who owns it and how often it changes.

- Every post exists to answer one question somebody would otherwise have to ask a person, not to describe a project
- Every post names an owner, so if it's wrong or out of date you know exactly who to go to
- Every post says how often it's updated, so you know whether to trust it as current
- One column, no categories or navigation beyond a plain list — nothing to configure before it's useful
- A post that stops saving anyone a question gets removed, not left to go stale

## What's on the page

A single list of posts, most recent first. No sections, no tags, no search — just the list.

Each post has:
- A short, plain title stating what it's about (not a headline, a fact — e.g. "Who owns the deploy pipeline")
- The body: what changed and what it meant, in short sentences
- An owner's name
- How often it's updated ("weekly", "when it changes", "monthly")

## Not decided yet

No real example was given in this sprint of an actual question this would have answered — that's the biggest gap. The next sprint should pin down two or three real posts (real names, real owner, real cadence) rather than a generic description like this one.

## Out of scope

No comments, no reactions, no notifications. Nobody subscribes to a post — you just come and check it.

## Look and feel

Follows the direction as given: white, sparse, a lot of air, navy headings, left-aligned, one column, flat panels, no shadows. Reads like something someone wrote and posted, not a designed feature.
