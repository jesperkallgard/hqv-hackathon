One page listing every area the team is responsible for, so nobody has to walk over and ask who's handling something.

- One page, one column, entries stacked top to bottom — no dashboard, no filters, no search.
- Every entry names one person as owner, not a team or a role.
- Every entry states how often it's updated — weekly, on change, monthly — so you know if it's stale before you trust it.
- Each entry says what changed most recently and what that meant, not a task log.
- It refuses to be a wiki: no nested pages, no categories, just the list.

Assumption made because it wasn't decided in the conversation: the group hadn't settled what the page actually lists. Going with "areas the team owns" (e.g. a system, a process, a recurring job) as the unit of an entry, since that's what fits the bar the facilitator set — a post that saves someone from asking a colleague, with a named owner and an update cadence.

Structure of one entry, in order:
- Heading: the name of the thing (plain, no product-speak).
- Owner: one person's name.
- Updated: how often — a stated cadence, not "recently".
- A few short sentences: what changed last and what it meant. Not what was done, what it means for anyone reading it.

Page-level:
- No landing copy, no intro paragraph explaining what the page is for. The list starts near the top.
- Order: not decided by this group — could be alphabetical, could be by how recently updated. Left open.

Look and feel: white background, a lot of air between entries, navy heading per entry, left-aligned, one column, flat panels with no shadow or border decoration. It should read like someone typed it up, not like it was designed.

Out of scope: no comments, no notifications, no edit history, no search. If an entry needs any of that, it's not a fit for this page.

Still open: which specific areas/things get an entry first. The group didn't get to naming them — that's the next thing to settle.
