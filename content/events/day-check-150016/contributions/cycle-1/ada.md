An internal site called What's Changed. It opens on a Monday morning with a header, a short navigation, and the feed first. The first view answers the common questions in one screen.

- The header carries the site name and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The feed sits under the header as the main view. It shows what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator, newest first.
- Each feed row names the change, the owner, and the date, so a post can save somebody from asking a colleague.
- The layout stays calm and open: white background, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels, no shadows, a footer that says who keeps the site.
- The site avoids dashboard treatment and charts. States are small round tags, and the buttons are fully round pills: orange with white text for the one primary action, outlined in navy for the rest.

Build a real internal website, not a document. Use a header with the site name and a short navigation, one generous content column, and a footer. Keep the look white, airy, left-aligned, with navy headings and flat panels with no shadows. Use fully round pill buttons only: one primary orange button with white text, and outlined navy pills for the rest. Use small round tags for states, not coloured words. The content is the whole picture of the site as described here: What's Changed, the feed, who owns what, how we work, and the footer naming who keeps it. Every post must name an owner and how often it is updated. The feed must show what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator, newest first. It should feel plain and concrete, with short sentences, and it should let somebody get an answer in one screen without scrolling. No dashboard and no charts.
