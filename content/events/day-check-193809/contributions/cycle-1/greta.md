A calm internal site for seeing what changed, who owns it, and whether it is stale. It is for people who need to ask one less colleague.

- Shows a newest-first feed of changes with the owner and last update date on every row.
- Includes a short header nav to the feed, ownership, and how the site works.
- Makes staleness obvious by showing when each item was last touched and by whom.
- Keeps the interface plain: white, navy headings, orange only on the primary action.
- Refuses dashboards and charts; the page is a feed, not an analytics view.

# What it has to do
- One internal website called **What's Changed**.
- Header with the site name and three links: **Feed**, **Who owns what**, **How we work**.
- The main view is a single generous column feed.
- Feed rows are sorted newest first.
- Each row shows:
  - the post title or summary,
  - the owner name,
  - the last update date,
  - and, where relevant, what changed and what it meant.
- Footer says who keeps the site.

# Content
- Use plain, concrete copy.
- Example feed items can include:
  - what changed this week,
  - what's being tested,
  - who owns the dealer locator.
- Every post names an owner and how often it is updated.
- The last touched information must be visible at a glance.

# Look and feel
- Real internal website, not a document.
- White background.
- A lot of air.
- Navy headings.
- Text left-aligned.
- Flat panels, no shadows.
- Buttons are fully round pills.
- Primary action: orange with white text.
- Secondary actions: navy outlined pills.
- States are small round tags, not coloured words.

# Out of scope
- No dashboard.
- No charts.
- No marketing language.
- No complex analytics views.
- No shadows or card chrome.

# Notes
- The key point is staleness: it should be obvious when something was last touched and by whom.
- This is the first picture of the site, so it should feel complete enough to judge as a whole.
