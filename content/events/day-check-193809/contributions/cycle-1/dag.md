An internal site for people who need the answer fast and do not want to ask a colleague. It shows what changed, who owns it, and how often it updates in one calm screen.

- A header names the site and gives three ways in: the feed, owners, and how we work.
- The feed is the main view, newest first, with one row per post.
- Each post shows what changed, the owner, and the update date.
- The site stays to one screen for the common case; no dashboard and no charts.
- The look is white, airy, and plain: navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels, and orange only on the primary button.

Build a real internal website, not a document. Use a header with the site name and short navigation, one generous content column, and a footer that says who keeps the site.

Content:
- Site name: “What’s Changed”.
- Navigation items: Feed, Who owns what, How we work.
- Feed entries must read as short, concrete updates: what changed this week, what is being tested, who owns the dealer locator.
- Each row in the feed shows an owner and a date.
- Every post must name an owner and how often it is updated, because that is what earns it a place.

Behaviour and scope:
- The feed is the default view.
- The common case should fit on one screen without scrolling.
- Do not include a dashboard.
- Do not include charts.
- Keep the tone plain and concrete. Short sentences. Say what changed and what it meant, not what was done.

Look and feel:
- White background with a lot of air.
- Navy headings.
- Left-aligned text.
- Flat panels with no shadows.
- Buttons are fully round pills.
- Primary button: orange with white text.
- Other buttons: outlined in navy.
- States are small round tags, not coloured words.

Out of scope:
- No marketing language.
- No dense admin layout.
- No extra views beyond the simple internal navigation.
