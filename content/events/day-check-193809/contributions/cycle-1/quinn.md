A calm internal site for people who need the latest answer without asking around. It shows the feed, who owns each thing, and how the site works, all in one place.

- A header names the site and gives three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The main view is a single feed of what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator, newest first.
- Every row shows an owner and a date, so each post can be traced to a person.
- A who-owns-what view makes ownership the thing you check first, not a detail buried in a post.
- The footer says who keeps the site, and the page stays plain, white, and quiet with navy headings and one orange pill button.

Build a real internal website, not a document. Use a header with the site name and short navigation for Feed, Who owns what, and How we work. Keep the content in one generous column with a footer.

Visual direction: white background, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text. Panels are flat with no shadows. Buttons are fully round pills. The primary action is orange with white text; all other buttons are outlined in navy. States are small round tags, not coloured words.

Content direction: plain, concrete copy. Short sentences. Say what changed and what it meant, not what was done. No marketing language.

Merge rule: a post earns its place if it saves somebody from asking a colleague. Every post names an owner and how often it is updated. The page should make ownership easy to scan and act on.

Scope: show the whole site as a single prototype, including header, feed, owners, how we work, and footer. No dashboard, no charts, no extra product areas.
