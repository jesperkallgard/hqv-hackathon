A calm internal site for people in other teams who need the answer without asking around. It shows what changed, who owns it, and how often it moves, so the room can read one place instead of chasing people.

- The site opens with the name, three links, and one feed, not a dashboard.
- The feed is newest first and each row shows the owner and the date.
- Every post says what changed, what it means, and what changed next.
- The site is for somebody from another team who needs no vocabulary from us.
- It has a header and footer, a lot of white space, navy headings, flat panels, and one orange primary pill button; no charts, no shadows.

- Internal website, not a document.
- Header: site name “What’s Changed” and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, how we work.
- Main content: one generous column with the feed as the primary view.
- Feed entries are newest first and include: what changed this week, what’s being tested, who owns the dealer locator, plus owner and date on every row.
- Footer: says who keeps the site.
- Visual direction: white, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text.
- Panels are flat with no shadows.
- Buttons: one primary orange pill with white text; all other buttons are outlined navy pills.
- States are small round tags, not coloured words.
- Tone: plain and concrete, short sentences, says what changed and what it meant; no marketing language.
- The site earns its place by saving somebody from asking a colleague.
- Every post names an owner and how often it is updated.
- Out of scope: dashboard, charts, and any extra vocabulary that an outsider would need to learn.
