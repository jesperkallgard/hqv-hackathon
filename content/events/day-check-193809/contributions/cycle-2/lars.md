An internal front door with four calm routes: What’s changed, What we’re testing, How we do things, and Who owns what. Each card tells you the last update date and the one action it wants from you, so an outsider can land, click, and know what to do next.

- The front door is four cards in one column, with no extra sections or feeds above them.
- The four cards are What’s changed, What we’re testing, How we do things, and Who owns what.
- Each card shows its last update date, so stale content is easy to spot at a glance.
- Each card says the one action it wants from you, so the next step is obvious without asking a colleague.
- An outsider can read the four cards and know where to click without learning the site first.

## What this front door must do
- Replace the wider feed-first landing page with a simple front door made of four cards only.
- Keep the layout as one generous column on white space, with a header and short navigation above and a footer below, matching the current internal-site look.
- The four cards must be the only entry points on the page: **What’s changed**, **What we’re testing**, **How we do things**, and **Who owns what**.
- Each card needs a visible last update date.
- Each card needs one clear action sentence that tells the user what to do next.

## Content
- Use the exact four area names above.
- The page still reads like a real internal website, not a document.
- Keep the copy plain and concrete.
- Do not add marketing language.
- Do not turn the cards into vague summaries; each one must say what it is for and the one action it wants.

## Look and feel
- White background, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text.
- Flat panels only; no shadows anywhere.
- Buttons remain fully round pills: orange with white text for the primary action, outlined in navy for the rest.
- States stay as small round tags, not coloured words.
- The page should feel calm, clear, and easy to scan quickly.

## Out of scope
- No extra cards.
- No feed list on the front door.
- No search, filters, or dashboards.
- No new content system.
- No decorative elements or promotional copy.

## Assumption
- The front door is a redesign of the current first screen, not a new site.
