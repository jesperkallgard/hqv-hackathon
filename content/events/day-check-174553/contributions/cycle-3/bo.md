A real internal website with a header, a short navigation, one generous column of content, and a footer. The feed shows what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator, newest first, with an owner and a date on each row.

- The site is called What's Changed and the header carries the site name.
- The header includes three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The feed is the main view and it is ordered newest first.
- Each row shows an owner and a date.
- The site has a footer that says who keeps the site.

White, a lot of air, navy headings, and left-aligned text. It should feel like a real internal website, not a document. Panels are flat, with no shadows anywhere. Every button is a fully round pill: orange with white text for the one primary action, outlined in navy for the rest. States are small round tags, not coloured words. It should sound plain and concrete, with short sentences that say what changed and what it meant, not what was done. No marketing language. Worth keeping means a post saves somebody from asking a colleague. Every post names an owner and how often it is updated. Open is the single entry: what one post carries, field by field, and what changes in a list versus when it is open. No dashboard and no charts.
