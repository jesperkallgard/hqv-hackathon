A real internal site opens on a Monday morning with a header, a short navigation, a single feed, and a footer. The first screen gives one screen’s worth of answers without scrolling.

- The site is called What's Changed.
- The header carries the site name and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The main view is the feed: what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator, newest first.
- Each row shows an owner and a date, so a person can see who to ask and how fresh the item is.
- The footer says who keeps the site, and the whole page stays calm, white, left-aligned, with navy headings and one orange primary button; no dashboard, no charts.

Build the site as an internal website, not a document. Use a header with the site name and a short navigation, one generous content column, and a footer. Keep the look white and airy, with navy headings and text left-aligned. Panels are flat with no shadows. Buttons are fully round pills: orange with white text for the one primary action, outlined in navy for the rest. States are small round tags, not coloured words. The content on the first screen is the feed plus the owner's name and update date on each item, so somebody can get an answer without asking a colleague. Every post earns its place by saving somebody from asking a colleague, and every post names an owner and how often it is updated. No dashboard, no charts, and nothing beyond the whole-picture first screen is decided here.
