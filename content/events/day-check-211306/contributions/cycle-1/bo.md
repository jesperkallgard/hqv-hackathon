A calm internal site for people who need to see what changed, who owns it, and what to ask next. It gives the whole picture in one place, so nobody has to chase a colleague for the basics.

- The home screen shows one feed first, with the newest items at the top.
- The site also gives direct paths to who owns what and how we work.
- Every item names an owner and the date it was last updated.
- State appears as small round tags, not as coloured words.
- It is a real internal website: header, short navigation, one generous column, and a footer.

Build a single internal website called **What's Changed**.

### Content and structure
- Header with the site name and three navigation links:
  - Feed
  - Who owns what
  - How we work
- Main content in one generous column.
- Footer that says who keeps the site.
- The landing view is the feed.
- The feed shows:
  - what changed this week
  - what is being tested
  - who owns the dealer locator
- Sort feed items newest first.
- Every row in the feed shows:
  - an owner
  - a date
- Every post on the page names an owner and how often it is updated.
- The page is about helping someone avoid asking a colleague.

### Look and feel
- White background.
- A lot of air.
- Navy headings.
- Text left-aligned.
- Panels are flat.
- No shadows anywhere.
- One primary action only, as a fully round orange pill with white text.
- All other buttons are fully round outlined navy pills.
- State should appear as small round tags, not coloured words.
- Plain, concrete copy.
- Short sentences.
- Say what changed and what it meant, not what was done.
- No marketing language.

### Out of scope
- No dashboard.
- No charts.
- No extra analytics views.
- No placeholder copy.

### Assumptions
- The site is a prototype for an internal team.
- If an owner or update frequency is unknown for a real item, leave it clearly unspecified rather than inventing it.
