When this exists and someone opens it on a Monday morning, they see a portal: clear entry points into different areas, and a header that shows KPIs at a glance.

- The homepage is a portal, not a document — it presents clear entry points into different areas of the organisation.
- The header displays KPIs, not just the site name and navigation.
- A post earns its place only if it saves someone from asking a colleague.
- Every post names an owner and how often it is updated.
- The look follows the set direction: white, airy, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels, pill buttons — orange primary, navy outline for the rest.

Content: the homepage must function as a portal with clearly labelled entry points into different areas — what those areas are was not specified. The header must show KPIs; which KPIs, how many, and how they update was not discussed. Every post needs a named owner and an update frequency shown alongside it, per the worth-keeping rule. Tone: plain and concrete, short sentences, says what changed and what it meant — no marketing language. Look: real internal website, not a document — header with site name and short navigation, one generous content column, footer; white background, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text; flat panels, no shadows; buttons as fully round pills, orange with white text for the single primary action, navy outline for the rest; status shown as small round tags, not coloured words. Open / not decided: what the 'different areas' of the portal actually are, what KPIs appear in the header and their data source, how the portal entry points relate to the individual posts and their owners, and how KPIs interact with the flat, shadow-free, pill-button visual language. Only one line of input was given for this team, so almost everything beyond the single portal-plus-KPI-header idea remains undecided.
