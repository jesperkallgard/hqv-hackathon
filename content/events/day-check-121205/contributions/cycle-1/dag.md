The whole homepage is one list, newest first, called What's changed. It replaces three questions people keep asking a colleague with one screen they can check themselves.

- The first screen is one list called "What's changed" — nothing else on it, no sidebar, no widgets.
- Every row is one line, plus an owner's name and a date. No long descriptions, no categories.
- There is no search box. You scan the list; you don't search it.
- Newest first, always — so the answer to "what happened this week" is always at the top, no scrolling to find it.
- The list mixes three kinds of thing on purpose: site changes, active tests, and ownership facts like who owns the dealer locator.

## What it is
A single page, single column, called "What's changed". It is the whole first screen — no tabs, no filters, no search box, no sidebar.

## The list
Newest first. Every row is one line, then the owner's name, then a date — always in that order, no exceptions.

Rows mix three kinds of entries in one feed, in the order they happened, not grouped by type:

- **Site changes** — something that was done to the site. e.g. "Homepage banner swapped to the autumn campaign — Owner: Dag — updated 12 Nov"
- **Active tests** — something being tried right now. e.g. "A/B test on the checkout button colour, live since 10 Nov — Owner: Ada — updated 10 Nov"
- **Ownership facts** — who owns a fixed piece of the site, and how often that is checked. e.g. "Dealer locator — Owner: Sven — reviewed monthly, last checked 1 Nov"

The three example rows above are illustrative, made up for this hand-in — the real first rows will be whatever Dag and Ada actually know changed this week. That's the one gap: real names and dates need to go in before this is real.

## The rule that earns a merge
Every row names an owner and says how often that thing is updated or checked. A row with no owner or no cadence does not belong on the list.

## Deliberate refusals
- No search box. The group's reasoning: if you can search, you only look for what you already expect to find, and you stop reading the rest. Scanning is the point — you see things you weren't looking for.
- No categories or tabs splitting the three kinds of entry apart. They stay mixed, one feed, because the point is one place to look, not three.
- No comments, no notifications, no email digest. Just the list.

## Look and feel
White background, a lot of air between rows. Navy for the page title "What's changed" and nothing else coloured. Flat panels, no shadows, no cards with borders trying to look like a product tile — it should read like a page someone typed up, not a page someone designed. Left-aligned, one column throughout.
