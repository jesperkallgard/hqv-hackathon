## What the room decided

Both teams built the same page, and that agreement is the spine: one list, newest first, called "What's changed". Nothing above it, no sidebar, no dashboard tiles, no tabs. Every row is one line, an owner, and a date, always in that order. The three kinds of entry — site changes, active tests, ownership facts — stay mixed in one feed, in the order they happened, because the point is one place to look, not three.

There is no search box. Team Dag's reasoning is the one on the page: if you can search, you only look for what you already expect to find, and you stop reading the rest. Scanning is the point — you see things you weren't looking for. Team Bo put the same rule the other way up: if it isn't findable by reading down the list, it doesn't belong on the list.

The bar to get on the list is Team Dag's and Team Bo's shared rule, and it is printed at the foot of the list rather than left in a brief: a row only earns its place if it names an owner and says how often it is updated. Team Bo wanted nothing whatsoever above the list — no welcome text, no summary, no counts. The page keeps one short line under the title saying what the list is, because the page has to explain itself to somebody arriving from a link with no one to ask; that is the one place this build went with Dag's framing over Bo's flat refusal, and it is the first thing to cut if the room disagrees.

Team Bo's refusal of a badge or a category label is kept exactly. Whether a row is a change, a test or an ownership fact comes from what the line says. No tags, no filter chips, no "view by owner" toggle, no comments, no reactions, no notifications, no email digest.

## What was filled in

The rows are examples. The room wrote three illustrative rows each and said plainly that the real ones are whatever the team actually knows changed this week; twelve rows are here so the page can be read as a page. Where the room's examples named a colleague, the page names the team or the role instead — an invented row about a real person is the one thing on this page nobody could check. The cadence text on each row, the dates, and the footer line are also filled in.

## Left open

Who can add a row, and whether old rows ever drop off the bottom, was not settled this sprint. The "Add a row" button sits where it would sit and does nothing. Real names and dates need to go in before the list is real.
