One list, newest first, called "What's changed." It answers what changed this week, what's being tested, and who owns the dealer locator — because they're all just rows.

- The whole first screen is one list titled "What's changed," newest first, and nothing else — no tabs, no filters, no categories.
- Every row is one line: the change, the owner's name, and the date.
- "Who owns the dealer locator" is answered by a row in the same list, not a separate page or a pinned banner.
- Staleness is answered by the row itself: it says how often that thing gets updated, so a row from six weeks ago that's only touched "when it changes" still reads as current, not forgotten.
- There is no search box. If a row matters, it gets re-posted and moves back to the top — that's the only way anything stays findable.

## What it is
A single page. Title: "What's changed." Below it, one column of rows, newest first. Nothing above the list except the title. No search, no filters, no tabs, no categories.

## A row
One line of text, plus an owner's name and a date. That's the whole row — no body copy, no expand.

Example rows (real content, not placeholders):
- "Homepage hero swapped to the autumn campaign. — Ada, updated weekly"
- "New checkout A/B test live, 10% of traffic. — Bo, updated as it runs"
- "Dealer locator. Owner: Dag. Updated only when ownership changes."

Note the third row: it's not "news," it's a fact. It sits in the same list as the news because that's how the ownership question gets answered without a separate page — you scan the same list you'd check for anything else.

## Why staleness isn't a problem
Every row names how often the thing behind it changes. A row that says "updated weekly" and hasn't moved in two weeks looks wrong. A row that says "updated only when ownership changes" and hasn't moved in two months looks correct. The cadence is what tells you whether to trust an old row — that's the whole mechanism, and it's why nothing needs to be pinned.

## Look and feel
Matches the direction as given: white, sparse, a lot of air. Navy heading for the title. Left-aligned, one column. Flat rows, no shadows, no cards with borders trying to look important. It should read like a page someone typed, not a dashboard someone designed.

## Explicitly out of scope
- No search box.
- No categories or tags on rows.
- No pinning — the dealer locator row is not visually different from any other row.
- No comments, reactions, or read receipts.
- No pagination decision made yet — how far back the list goes is open.
