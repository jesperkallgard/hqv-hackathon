The front page of the site — not a post, the list that gets you to one. Written from a short conversation: Dag knew it should be "something for the team" but hadn't fixed what's on it, so this is our reading of the smallest useful version.

- It's an index, not a post — it lists what exists and gets you to one, it doesn't say anything itself.
- Four sections, no more: Playbooks, Test Results, Rollout Status, Decisions — the same four things the bar says earn a place.
- Every entry shows its title, its owner, and how often it's updated. No entry appears without both.
- A section with nothing in it yet is still named and still shown — empty, not hidden.
- No entry carries a description beyond what changed and what it meant. Nothing that only proves someone was busy.

## What it is
The landing page of the DBS internal site. One page, one column, four sections stacked top to bottom: Playbooks, Test Results, Rollout Status, Decisions. Each section is a flat panel — no shadow, no card styling — holding a short list of entries.

Each entry is one line: **title — owner — update cadence**. Example shape (not real content, just the pattern): "Sticky nav test — Ada, CRO — updated weekly". No further blurb on this page; you click through for the actual post.

## Look
White background, a lot of air between sections. Navy for the four section headings. Left-aligned throughout, no centring, no hero, no imagery. It should read like a written index, not a designed homepage.

## What's deliberately out
No search box, no filters, no sort options, no dates beyond the cadence label. No entry without an owner — if there's no owner yet, the entry doesn't go up, per the bar.

## Open / assumed
Dag hadn't decided what actually goes on the page yet — the four section names are lifted straight from the facilitator's bar (playbooks, test results, rollout status, decisions), not invented. No real entries are specified yet; this sprint settles the shape, not the content. That's the next thing to fill in.
