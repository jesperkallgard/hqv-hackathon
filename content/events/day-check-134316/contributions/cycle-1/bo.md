One page, one table: every rollout in flight, what stage it's at, and who owns it — so nobody has to ask.

- One page, one table — no per-rollout writeups, no subpages
- Four stages only: Testing, Rolling out, Live everywhere, Paused — nothing in between
- Every row names an owner and a last-updated date, not a percentage or an ETA
- If a row hasn't been touched in a while it says so, instead of quietly going stale
- Refuses to hold test results or decisions — that's a different post, this one is just where things stand

Group's starting brief was thin — "something for the team, a page, probably, not sure what goes on it yet." This hand-in is our reading of the smallest thing that fits the bar: rollout status is named directly in the facilitator's brief as something worth posting, and "where are we with X" is the exact question this bar wants a page to kill.

**What's on the page:**
A single table, one column of content, nothing else on the page above it but the title and one line saying what it's for.

Columns: Rollout name | Stage | Owner | Last updated.

Stage is one of exactly four words: Testing, Rolling out, Live everywhere, Paused. No percentages, no target dates, no "on track" language.

Example rows (placeholder structure only — real rollouts to be filled in by whoever owns this once built):
- Sticky nav on PDP — Rolling out — Owner: [name] — Last updated: [date]
- New checkout flow — Testing — Owner: [name] — Last updated: [date]

**Look:** matches the direction exactly — white background, navy header row on the table, alternating light grey rows, no shadows, no dashboard chrome. One column, left-aligned. The table is the whole page; it should look like a doc someone typed up, not a tool.

**Refuses:**
- No charts, no progress bars, no percentages.
- No test-result detail, no reasoning for decisions — that content belongs on a different kind of post, not this one.
- No notifications or subscriptions.

**Open / not decided:** the group didn't get further than this in the time available — real rollout names, real owners, and how often "last updated" gets checked are not yet known and shouldn't be invented.
