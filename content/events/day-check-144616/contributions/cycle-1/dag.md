A single internal site called What's Changed opens on Monday morning with a header, three ways in, and one main feed. It shows what changed, who owns it, and what needs a colleague answer fast.

- The header carries the site's name and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The main content is one generous feed, newest first, with rows for what changed this week, what's being tested, and who owns the dealer locator.
- Every row names an owner and a date, and each item carries a small round status tag.
- The one primary action is a fully round orange button with white text for posting an update; the other actions are outlined navy pill buttons.
- A footer says who keeps the site, and there are no dashboards, charts, or other extra sections.

Build a real internal website, not a document. It uses a header with the site's name and a short navigation, one generous content column, and a footer. The look is white, airy, left-aligned, with navy headings and flat panels with no shadows. Buttons are fully round pills: orange with white text for the one primary action, outlined navy for the rest. States are small round tags, not coloured words. The content is plain and concrete, with short sentences that say what changed and what it meant, not what was done. Worth keeping means it saves somebody from asking a colleague. Every post must name an owner and how often it is updated. The site is centered on ownership; there is no dashboard or chart.
