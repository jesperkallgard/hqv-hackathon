A real internal site called What’s Changed opens on Monday morning with a header, a short nav, one main feed, and a footer. It feels calm and plain, and it gets somebody to an answer in one screen.

- The header shows the site name and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The feed is the main thing on the page. It shows what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator, with the newest items first.
- Each item in the feed carries an owner and a date, so the page answers who is responsible and when it changed without asking a colleague.
- The page keeps the look simple: white space, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels with no shadows, and states shown as small round tags.
- The one orange pill button is the single primary action on the front door; everything else is an outlined navy pill, and the footer says who keeps the site.

Build an internal website, not a document. It needs a header with the site name and short navigation, one generous column of content, and a footer. The site name is What’s Changed. The navigation includes the feed, who owns what, and how we work. The main content is a feed of updates, newest first, with each row showing an owner and a date. The feed should surface what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator. It should feel calm, white, and airy, with navy headings and left-aligned text. Panels are flat and have no shadows. Buttons are fully round pills: one primary orange button with white text, and the rest outlined in navy. States are small round tags, not coloured words. The site should let somebody get an answer in one screen without scrolling. Every post must name an owner and how often it is updated. No dashboard, no charts, and no extra page types are in scope.
