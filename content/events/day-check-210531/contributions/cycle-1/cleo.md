A calm internal homepage that shows the whole site at once. People open it to see what changed, who owns it, and how often it is updated, without asking a colleague.

- It is one internal site with a header, a feed, an ownership view, a how-we-work view, and a footer.
- The header has the site name and short navigation: Feed, Owners, and How we work.
- The feed leads the page and shows what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator.
- Every item shows a human owner and an update date, so the answer is visible without a follow-up.
- The look is white, spacious, navy, and flat, with orange only for the primary pill button and small round tags for states.

Build the whole site as a real internal website, not a document.

## Structure
- Header with the site name: **What’s Changed**.
- Short navigation: **Feed**, **Owners**, **How we work**.
- Main content in one generous column.
- Footer that says who keeps the site.

## What the site shows
- The feed is the primary view.
- It shows real posts about:
  - what changed this week
  - what is being tested
  - who owns the dealer locator
- The site also includes an owners view and a how we work view.
- The feed is ordered newest first.
- Every post shows:
  - the owner as a person
  - the date
  - the update frequency: weekly, monthly, or static

## What each post must say
- What changed.
- What it meant.
- Not what was done.
- A post only earns space if it helps somebody avoid asking a colleague.

## Look and feel
- White background.
- Lots of air.
- Navy headings.
- Left-aligned text.
- Flat panels, no shadows.
- Orange fully round pill for the primary action.
- Outlined navy pills for the rest.
- Statuses are small round tags, not coloured words.

## Copy
- Plain and concrete.
- Short sentences.
- No marketing language.
- No internal jargon in the headline.

## Out of scope
- No dashboard.
- No charts.
- No analytics tooling.
- No multi-step workflow.
- No placeholder content.
