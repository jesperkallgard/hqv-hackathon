A one-screen internal homepage for people who need the answer fast. It shows what changed this week, who owns it, and how often it updates, so nobody has to ask a colleague.

- The whole page fits on one screen with the feed visible at once; no dashboard, no charts, no long scroll.
- The header names the site and gives three links: Feed, Ownership, and How we work.
- The feed is newest first and every row shows the change, the owner, and the update date.
- Each post says what changed and what it meant, not what was done, and each one names a person as owner.
- The look is calm and sparse: white background, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels, orange only for the primary pill button.

Build a real internal website, not a document.

## Content
- Site name: **What’s Changed**.
- Header navigation: **Feed**, **Ownership**, **How we work**.
- Feed items shown in the first screen without scrolling.
- Example feed content should feel like this:
  - what changed this week
  - what’s being tested
  - who owns the dealer locator
- Each row shows:
  - a human owner name
  - a date
  - the update frequency: **weekly**, **monthly**, or **static**.
- Footer copy: **Who keeps the site**.

## Behaviour
- Feed is sorted newest first.
- The page’s job is to save somebody from asking a colleague.
- Do not add a dashboard.
- Do not add charts.
- Do not make the user scroll to get the first answer.

## Look and feel
- White background.
- Lots of air.
- Navy headings.
- Text left-aligned.
- Flat panels only; no shadows.
- Primary action is a fully round orange pill with white text.
- Secondary actions are outlined navy pills.
- Statuses are small round tags, not coloured words.

## Copy style
- Plain and concrete.
- Short sentences.
- Say what changed and what it meant.
- No marketing language.
- No internal jargon in headlines.

## Out of scope
- No charts.
- No analytics dashboard.
- No multi-step workflows.
- No placeholder copy.
