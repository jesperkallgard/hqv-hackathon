An internal site for asking what changed, who owns it, and how the work moves. It is for anyone who wants the answer without chasing a colleague.

- A header carries the site name and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The feed is the front door: newest first, with what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator.
- Every item shows an owner and a date, so the right person is always visible.
- A short how-we-work page explains the rules in plain language and keeps the tone concrete.
- A footer says who keeps the site, and the whole page stays calm, white, and easy to scan.

Build a real internal website, not a document. Make it feel like an internal tool people use every day.

Structure:
- Header with the site name and short navigation to Feed, Who owns what, and How we work.
- One generous content column.
- Footer.

Look and feel:
- White background, lots of air.
- Navy headings.
- Left-aligned text.
- Flat panels, no shadows.
- Buttons are fully round pills.
- Orange with white text for the one primary action.
- Navy outlined pills for the rest.
- States are small round tags, not coloured words.

Content:
- Feed shows what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator.
- Sort newest first.
- Every row must show an owner and a date.
- The how-we-work page uses short, plain sentences.
- Say what changed and what it meant, not what was done.
- Footer says who keeps the site.

Rules:
- No dashboard.
- No charts.
- No marketing language.
- Every visible thing should help someone avoid asking a colleague.
- If an item does not have an owner or update cadence, leave that gap visible rather than inventing it.
