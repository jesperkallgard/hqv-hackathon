One internal site for asking what changed and who owns it. It is for people who need the answer fast and want to know who to ask next.

- A header names the site and gives three ways in: the feed, ownership, and how it works.
- The main view is a newest-first feed of changes this week, tests, and owned areas like the dealer locator.
- Every row shows an owner and a date, so no item is anonymous.
- The page refuses charts and dashboards; it stays a single calm column with a footer.
- The one thing it cares most about is ownership: every visible part names a person you can go and ask.

Build a real internal website, not a document. Use a header with the site name and short navigation links for Feed, Who owns what, and How we work. Put the content in one generous column with a footer.

Look and feel: white background, lots of air, navy headings, text left-aligned. Panels are flat with no shadows. Every button is a fully round pill: orange with white text for the primary action, outlined in navy for the rest. States are small round tags, not coloured words.

Content to include: the feed shows what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator. Sort newest first. Every row must show an owner and a date. The footer says who keeps the site.

Rules: no dashboard, no charts. Keep the tone plain and concrete, with short sentences. Say what changed and what it meant, not what was done. Every single thing on the page must name a person you can go and ask. If an item does not have an owner, do not invent one; leave the gap visible.
