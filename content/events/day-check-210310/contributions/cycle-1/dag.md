An internal site for anyone who needs to know what changed without asking a colleague. It shows the feed first, then who owns what and how we work, in plain language.

- The home page is a single feed of changes, newest first.
- Each row shows what changed, who owns it, and when it was updated.
- A separate view shows who owns what, so nobody has to ask around.
- A short how-we-work page explains the rhythm of updates and what each tag means.
- There is no dashboard or charting layer; the site stays simple and readable for people outside the team.

# What it has to do
- Present one internal website called **What's Changed**.
- Top level navigation: **Feed**, **Who owns what**, **How we work**.
- The feed leads the site and is the default landing view.
- Feed items are ordered newest first.
- Each feed row names:
  - the change
  - an owner
  - a date
  - how often it is updated
- The site exists to save somebody from asking a colleague.
- The main audience is somebody from another team who does not know the local vocabulary.

# Content
- Use plain, concrete copy.
- Headings in navy.
- Short, direct sentences.
- Say what changed and what it meant.
- Avoid marketing language.
- Footer copy should say who keeps the site.
- Keep all labels understandable to an outsider.

# Look and feel
- Real internal website, not a document.
- White background, lots of air.
- One generous column for content.
- Flat panels, no shadows.
- Left-aligned text.
- Navy headings.
- Buttons are fully round pills.
- Primary button: orange with white text.
- Secondary buttons: outlined in navy.
- Statuses are small round tags, not coloured words.

# Deliberately out of scope
- No dashboard.
- No charts.
- No complex analytics.
- No extra product sections beyond the three navigation areas.

# Assumption
- The feed is the most important entry point and should carry the first-screen weight.
