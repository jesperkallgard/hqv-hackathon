A simple internal site that shows what changed, who owns it, and how it is kept up to date. It is for people who need the answer fast without asking a colleague.

- The site opens on a feed of changes, newest first.
- Every post shows what changed, who owns it, and when it was updated.
- The site also shows who owns what, so ownership is easy to find.
- A short how-we-work section explains the update rhythm and the meaning of the tags.
- A small footer says who keeps the site, and there is no dashboard or chart layer.

# What it has to do
- Build a real internal website called **What's Changed**.
- The page is a whole site, not a document.
- Header: site name plus a short navigation.
- Navigation links: **Feed**, **Who owns what**, **How we work**.
- Footer: says who keeps the site.
- The feed is the main view and should be the first thing people see.
- The site should answer questions without making people ask a colleague.
- Every post must name an owner and how often it is updated.

# Feed
- Show what changed this week and what is being tested.
- Keep the feed newest first.
- Each row shows:
  - the change
  - the owner
  - the date
  - the update frequency
- A post earns its place if it helps somebody avoid asking around.

# Who owns what
- Show ownership in a simple list or directory.
- Make it easy to find the person or team responsible.
- Keep the labels plain and concrete.

# How we work
- Explain how updates happen.
- Explain what the tags mean.
- Keep the explanation short.
- Write it for somebody outside the team.

# Content and tone
- Plain and concrete.
- Short sentences.
- Say what changed and what it meant.
- No marketing language.
- No local jargon unless the site explains it.
- Headings in navy.
- Text left-aligned.

# Look and feel
- White background.
- Lots of air.
- One generous content column.
- Flat panels.
- No shadows anywhere.
- Buttons are fully round pills.
- Primary button: orange with white text.
- Other buttons: outlined in navy.
- Statuses are small round tags, not coloured words.

# Out of scope
- No dashboard.
- No charts.
- No analytics view.
- No extra sections beyond the three navigation areas.
