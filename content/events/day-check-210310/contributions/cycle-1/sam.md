A simple internal site for seeing what changed, who owns it, and how to work. It helps people get an answer in one screen without asking a colleague.

- A header names the site and gives three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The first screen is the feed, newest first, so people can see what changed this week without hunting.
- Each row shows what changed, who owns it, and the date, so the answer is always attached to a person and a time.
- A short owners view makes it easy to find who keeps each thing up to date.
- A plain how-we-work view explains the rules in short, concrete language, and the footer says who keeps the site.

Build a real internal website, not a document. Keep it to one generous column with a header, short nav, main content, and footer.

Content and structure:
- Site name: “What’s Changed”.
- Header navigation: Feed, Who owns what, How we work.
- Feed is the main first view and the default landing area.
- Feed items are ordered newest first.
- Feed rows should show: what changed, owner, date.
- Include a view for ownership: who owns what.
- Include a view for how we work.
- Footer text should say who keeps the site.

Look and feel:
- White background with a lot of air.
- Navy headings.
- Left-aligned text.
- Flat panels, no shadows anywhere.
- One primary action only, styled as a fully round orange pill with white text.
- All other buttons are outlined navy pills.
- Statuses are small round tags, not coloured words.

Tone:
- Plain and concrete.
- Short sentences.
- Say what changed and what it meant, not what was done.
- No marketing language.

Scope notes:
- No dashboard.
- No charts.
- Keep the first screen simple enough that somebody gets their answer without scrolling.
