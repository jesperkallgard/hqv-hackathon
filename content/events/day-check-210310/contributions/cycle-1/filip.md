An internal site for asking who owns what, what changed, and how often it moves. It is for people who need the answer fast without asking a colleague.

- A header names the site and gives three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The feed is the main view, newest first, with each row showing what changed, who owns it, and the date.
- A who owns what section makes ownership obvious for every post and every area on the site.
- A how we work section says posts earn their place by saving someone from asking a colleague.
- A footer says who keeps the site up to date.

Build a real internal website, not a document.

Content and structure:
- Site name: “What’s Changed”.
- Header with the site name and a short navigation to: Feed, Who owns what, How we work.
- Main content is one generous column.
- Feed is the primary view.
- Feed items are sorted newest first.
- Each feed row must show:
  - what changed
  - who owns it
  - the date
  - how often it is updated
- The page must also include a clear section for who owns what.
- The page must also include a short how we work section.
- Footer says who keeps the site.

Content tone:
- Plain and concrete.
- Short sentences.
- Say what changed and what it meant.
- No marketing language.

Look and feel:
- White background.
- A lot of air.
- Navy headings.
- Text left-aligned.
- Flat panels.
- No shadows anywhere.
- Buttons are fully round pills.
- Primary button: orange with white text.
- Other buttons: outlined in navy.
- States are small round tags, not coloured words.

Scope:
- No dashboard.
- No charts.
- No extra visual systems.
- Keep it feeling like a real internal website.

Ownership:
- Every single thing on the page must name a person the user can go and ask.
- Posts only earn their place if they save somebody from asking a colleague.
