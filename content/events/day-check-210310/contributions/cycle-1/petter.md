An internal site for checking what changed, who owns it, and how to work without asking around. It is for people who need the answer fast and want the site to stay calm, plain, and useful.

- A header names the site and gives three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The feed is the main view, with newest items first and each row showing the owner and the date.
- Every post says what changed this week, what is being tested, or who owns a thing like the dealer locator.
- The site uses white space, navy headings, flat panels, and one orange pill button; nothing casts a shadow.
- It refuses dashboards and charts, and keeps the page down to the few things that stop someone asking a colleague.

Build a single internal website called **What's Changed**.

### Content
- Header: site name plus three navigation items:
  - Feed
  - Who owns what
  - How we work
- Main view: the feed.
- Feed entries should read like internal updates: what changed this week, what is being tested, who owns the dealer locator.
- Each row shows:
  - title or short update text
  - owner name
  - date
- Footer: a simple line saying who keeps the site.

### Look and feel
- Real internal website, not a document.
- One generous content column.
- White background.
- Lots of air.
- Navy headings.
- Left-aligned text.
- Flat panels only.
- No shadows anywhere.
- Buttons are fully round pills.
- Primary action: orange with white text.
- Other actions: outlined in navy.
- Statuses are small round tags, not coloured words.
- Calm and restrained.

### Scope
- No dashboard.
- No charts.
- No extra navigation beyond the three items above.
- Keep the page sparse; fewer things, each doing more work.

### Copy tone
- Plain and concrete.
- Short sentences.
- Say what changed and what it meant.
- No marketing language.

### Notes
- The page should feel like a useful internal tool, not a report or presentation.
- The feed should be readable at a glance and feel like the first thing someone lands on when they need an answer.
