One post for people who need to know where the login went and who to ask. It shows the change, who owns it, and when it is updated, so nobody has to ask a colleague.

- The opened post shows one headline, one audience, a three-line summary, a status or date, an owner by name, an update frequency, and one link onward.
- In the list view, only the headline, the owner, and the date are visible.
- The open view puts the three-line summary first, then the status or date, then the owner and update cadence.
- Every thing on the page names a person you can ask, and the owner is the clearest answer.
- The page stays a single internal screen with a header, short navigation, one generous column, and a footer.

Build a single internal page, not a document, with the day’s visual direction: white background, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels with no shadows, and pill buttons only where buttons are needed. Keep the header with the site name and a short navigation, then one generous column, then a footer.

This sprint is about one opened post. The post title is exactly: **My Husqvarna login moved**. The owner is **Jonas B**. The update frequency is **monthly**.

The opened post must contain exactly these seven pieces of information:
- headline
- audience
- a three-line summary
- a status or date
- an owner by name
- how often it is updated
- one link onward

In the list view, show only the headline, the owner, and the date. The rest appears only when the post is opened.

Content should be plain and concrete. Short sentences. Say what changed and what it meant. No marketing language. Use real names and avoid filler. The ownership cue matters most: every piece on the page should name a person the reader can go to.

Keep it to one post. No feed, no dashboard, no extra layer before the post. No shadows, no coloured-word statuses, and no extra page types.

The one link onward can be a simple internal link label for the next place to go, but it should stay part of the same post, not a separate workflow.
