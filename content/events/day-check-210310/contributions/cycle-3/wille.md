Open a single post from the list and keep the list lean. People see the headline, owner and date first, then the full update when they click in.

- The list shows only the headline, the owner name, and the update date.
- Opening the post reveals the audience, a three-line summary, status or date, owner, update rhythm, and one onward link.
- Every post keeps one named owner and one clear update frequency.
- The page stays restrained: fewer things on the page, with each thing doing more work.
- There is no feed, dashboard, or extra layer before the post opens.

## What this sprint changes
Make the first screen a simple list of posts, and make each post open to a fuller page. The list is the front door; the opened post is where the detail lives.

## Content that must exist
- Post headline
- Owner name
- Update date, shown in the list
- Audience, inside the post
- Three-line summary inside the post
- Status or date inside the post
- Update frequency inside the post
- One onward link inside the post

## List rules
- In the list, show only:
  - headline
  - owner
  - date
- Keep the list lean.
- Do not show the full summary, audience, or onward link in the list.
- No feed, no dashboard, no extra layer before opening a post.

## Open-post rules
- Opening a post reveals the rest of the information.
- The post should answer what changed and what it meant.
- Each post must name an owner.
- Each post must say how often it is updated.
- Each post must include one link onward.

## Tone
Plain and concrete.
Short sentences.
Say what changed and what it meant.
Do not use marketing language.

## Look and feel
- Real internal website, not a document.
- Header with the site name and short navigation.
- One generous column.
- Footer.
- White background.
- Lots of air.
- Navy headings.
- Left-aligned text.
- Flat panels, no shadows.
- Buttons are fully round pills.
- Primary action: orange with white text.
- Other buttons: outlined in navy.
- States are small round tags, not coloured words.

## Out of scope
- No feed view.
- No dashboard view.
- No extra navigation layer.
- No expanding content inside the list itself.
- No extra copy beyond the content above.
