A post that tells Husqvarna users what moved, who owns it, and what to do next. It is for people who need the answer without asking a colleague.

- The list shows the headline, the owner, and the date.
- Opening the post shows audience, a three-line summary, status or date, update frequency, and one link onward.
- The post tells you the one next action it wants from you.
- It keeps the copy plain and concrete, with short sentences and no marketing language.
- It stays a single opened post, not a feed or dashboard.

Build the opened view for the first post on What’s Changed.

Use this exact title: My Husqvarna login moved.
Use this exact owner: Jonas B.
Use this exact update frequency: updated monthly.

The page should still fit the current internal-site direction: white background, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text, a header with the site name and short navigation, one generous content column, and a footer. Panels are flat with no shadows. Buttons are fully round pills: orange with white text for the primary action and outlined navy for the rest. Statuses are small round tags, not coloured words.

This sprint is only about one opened post.

The open post must contain:
- Headline
- Audience
- A three-line summary
- Status or date
- Owner by name
- How often it is updated
- One link onward
- The one action it wants from you

In the list, show only the headline, owner, and date. The other details appear when the post is opened.

Tone:
- Plain and concrete.
- Short sentences.
- Say what changed and what it meant.
- No marketing language.

The point of the post is to save somebody from asking a colleague.

Do not add a feed, dashboard, or extra layers.
