One post opens with the headline, audience, summary, status or date, owner, update rhythm, and one next link. It is for people who need the action now, not the whole archive.

- The list shows only the headline, the owner, and the date.
- Opening a post reveals audience, a three-line summary, status or date, update frequency, and one onward link.
- Every post has one clear next action, so the page tells you what to do next.
- The content stays plain and concrete, with short lines and no filler.
- It is a single internal post view, not a feed, dashboard, or document.

Build the opened state for one post on the What’s Changed site.

The page should still feel like a real internal website: header with site name and short nav, one generous content column, footer. White background, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels, no shadows. Buttons are fully round pills: orange with white text for the primary action, outlined navy for the rest. Statuses are small round tags, not coloured words.

This sprint is about the opened post only.

Content for the open view:
- Headline
- Audience
- Three-line summary
- Status or date
- Owner by name
- How often it is updated
- One link onward
- One clear action the entry wants from you

List view rule:
- In the list, show only the headline, owner, and date.
- The rest appears when the post is opened.

Tone:
- Plain and concrete.
- Short sentences.
- Say what changed and what it meant, not what was done.
- No marketing language.

The post must answer the question: what happens next?

Keep it to one post, opened. Do not add a feed, dashboard, or extra layer.
