One internal home page that shows what changed, who owns it, and how often it moves. It gives people the shortest path to the owner before they ask a colleague.

- The header has the site name and three plain links: What changed, Who owns what, and How we work.
- The feed is the first thing you see, newest first, with a short row for each update.
- Every row names an owner and the update rhythm so you can go straight to a person.
- The page keeps the look calm and sparse: white, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels, and round pill buttons.
- There is no dashboard feel, no charts, and no hidden second layer before the owner is visible.

Build a single internal website called What’s Changed.

Keep the shell from the canon: header, short navigation, one generous content column, and a footer. Use the direction exactly: white background, lots of air, navy headings, text left-aligned, flat panels with no shadows, and fully round pill buttons. The one primary action is orange with white text; all other buttons are outlined in navy. States are small round tags, not coloured words.

This sprint is about finding your way without our words, so the front door must do the work. The navigation labels should be exactly:
- What changed
- Who owns what
- How we work

The main view is the feed. Show it as the first thing on the page, sorted newest first. Each item needs a clear owner and a date, and the row should make it obvious what changed, who owns it, and how often it updates.

Use the following real content from the canon as the starting point for the page structure and wording style:
- Site name: What’s Changed
- Existing sections: What’s changed, What we’re testing, How we do things, Who owns what
- Footer keeper text: Kept up to date by Web operations.

Keep the tone plain and concrete. Short sentences. Say what changed and what it meant, not what was done. No marketing language.

Deliberately out of scope:
- No dashboard view
- No charts
- No second screen before the owner can be found
- No coloured status labels beyond the small round tags

Every visible item should help someone know who to ask. That is the point of the page.
