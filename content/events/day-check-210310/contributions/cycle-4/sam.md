A single internal site for people who need to know what changed, who owns it, and what to do next. It helps anyone in the company get from the update to the action without asking a colleague.

- The header shows What’s Changed and three ways in: the feed, who owns what, and how we work.
- The feed reads newest first and each row names the owner and the date.
- Every entry says what changed, what is being tested, and the one action it wants from you.
- The site stays calm and sparse: white background, navy headings, left-aligned text, orange only for the one primary button.
- There is no dashboard and no charts; the footer says who keeps the site up to date.

Build a real internal site called What’s Changed, not a document.

Use a header with the site name and a short nav: Feed, Who owns what, How we work.

The main column is generous and there is a footer.

Style:
- White background.
- Lots of air.
- Navy headings.
- Left-aligned text.
- Flat panels with no shadows.
- One primary action button only, fully round pill, orange with white text.
- Other buttons, if any, are outlined in navy.
- States are small round tags, not coloured words.

Content:
- Feed items are newest first.
- Each row shows owner and date.
- The feed includes things like: what changed this week, what is being tested, who owns the dealer locator.
- Every entry must name an owner and how often it is updated.
- Every entry must also tell the user the one action it wants from them.

Copy should be plain and concrete. Short sentences. Say what changed and what it meant, not what was done. No marketing language.

Out of scope:
- No dashboard.
- No charts.
- Do not turn this into a document.

Footer copy should say who keeps the site.

If there is a better-sounding company name or owner list, do not invent one; keep it to the words already used here unless the canon says otherwise.
