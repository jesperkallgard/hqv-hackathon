A simple internal site for seeing what changed, who owns it, and how to work. It helps people get an answer in one screen without asking a colleague.

- The header shows What’s Changed and a short navigation to the feed, who owns what, how we work, and the footer.
- The feed sits in one generous column and reads newest first.
- Each post shows what changed, what it means, an owner, and when it is updated.
- The site stays flat and calm: white background, navy headings, left-aligned text, and no shadows anywhere.
- The only strong colour is orange on the one primary button; other buttons are outlined in navy and states are small round tags.

Build the whole site as a real internal website, not a document.

Structure:
- Header with the site name.
- Short navigation for Feed, Who owns what, How we work.
- Main content in one generous column.
- Footer.

Style:
- White background.
- Lots of air.
- Navy headings.
- Left-aligned text.
- Flat panels.
- No shadows anywhere.
- One primary action button only, fully round pill, orange with white text.
- Any secondary buttons are outlined in navy.
- States are small round tags, not coloured words.

Content:
- The feed is newest first.
- Include posts such as what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator.
- Each post must show:
  - what changed,
  - what it means,
  - an owner,
  - how often it is updated.
- Every post should save somebody from asking a colleague.
- The “How we work” and “Who owns what” sections should be part of the site, not separate documents.
- The footer should say who keeps the site up to date.

Copy:
- Plain and concrete.
- Short sentences.
- Say what changed and what it meant, not what was done.
- No marketing language.

Out of scope:
- No dashboard.
- No charts.
- No document-style layout.
- Do not invent extra pages or flows beyond the site picture above.
