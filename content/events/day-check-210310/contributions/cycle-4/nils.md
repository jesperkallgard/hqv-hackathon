A simple internal site for finding the answer without asking a colleague. It shows what changed, who owns it, and how often it is updated.

- A header names the site and gives four ways in: What’s changed, What we’re testing, How we do things, and Who owns what.
- The feed is the front door. It shows the latest changes first, with the thing that changed, the owner, and the date on every row.
- Each row earns its place by answering one question a colleague would otherwise get asked: what changed, what is being tested, or who owns it.
- The site includes a clear page for who owns what and a clear page for how we work, so people do not have to know the right team before they start.
- A footer says who keeps the site up to date, so the ownership is visible from the bottom of the page as well.

Build a real internal website, not a document. Keep the page one generous column on white with a lot of air, navy headings, text left-aligned, flat panels, and no shadows anywhere.

The header must show the site name, What’s Changed, and a short navigation with four links: What’s changed, What we’re testing, How we do things, and Who owns what.

The main feed is the core of the page. It should read newest first and show the post title, owner name, and update date on every row. The feed should include items such as what changed this week, what is being tested, and who owns the dealer locator. The rows should be easy to scan and should not rely on insider vocabulary.

The audience is someone from another team. The writing has to help them find the right thing without asking a colleague. Use plain, concrete sentences. Say what changed and what it meant, not what was done. No marketing language.

Every post on the site must name an owner and say how often it is updated. That information needs to be visible on the row and in the page content.

The feed should not include dashboards or charts. The page should stay simple and editorial.

The primary action, if any, should be an orange fully round pill with white text. Other actions should be outlined navy pills. States should use small round tags, not coloured words.

Include a footer that says who keeps the site up to date.

Do not add extra sections beyond the header, feed, who owns what, how we do things, and footer.
