The front door is four cards in one column. An outsider can click into what changed, what is being tested, how we do things, and who owns what, and every card names a person you can ask.

- The front door is exactly four areas: What’s changed, What we’re testing, How we do things, and Who owns what.
- The page uses four cards in one column so the next click is obvious.
- Each card shows the last update date, so the page itself tells you how fresh it is.
- Every single thing on the page names a person you can go and ask.
- The front door puts ownership first, because the main job is to help people find the right colleague fast.

Build the front door for What’s Changed as a real internal website, not a document.

Keep the canon’s site shape: white, a lot of air, navy headings, left-aligned text, flat panels, no shadows. Keep the header with the site name and short navigation, and the footer.

This sprint changes the front door only.

Structure:
- Four cards in one column.
- The four areas are exactly:
  1. What’s changed
  2. What we’re testing
  3. How we do things
  4. Who owns what
- An outsider should understand where to click from those four cards alone.
- Each card shows the last update date.
- Every card and every item on the page must name a person you can ask.

Content:
- Keep the page concrete and plain.
- The key promise is ownership: every visible item needs a named person.
- Do not add extra areas or a dashboard.
- Do not add charts.

Tone:
- Short, direct sentences.
- No marketing language.
- Say what it is for, not what the team did.

Look and controls:
- One primary action only, as an orange fully round pill with white text.
- Other buttons stay outlined navy pills.
- Statuses stay as small round tags, not coloured words.

Out of scope:
- No new sections beyond the four cards.
- No multi-column layout.
- No extra summary widgets.
