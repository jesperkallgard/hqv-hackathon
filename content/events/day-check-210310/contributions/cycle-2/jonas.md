Four cards take you to the part you need without knowing our language. An outsider can land, spot the right place, and move on.

- The front door shows four cards only: What’s changed, What we’re testing, How we do things, and Who owns what.
- Each card is a plain entry point to its section, with the last update date shown on the card.
- The cards are written for people outside the team, so the labels are enough on their own.
- Nothing else sits on the front door: no feed, no extra highlights, no second layer of choices.
- The page still feels like a real internal website: white, airy, navy headings, flat cards, and round pills for actions.

Build the front door as a one-column internal page with a header, short nav, and footer, matching the shared look and tone.

Content:
- Title: What’s Changed
- Four cards exactly, in this order:
  1. What’s changed
  2. What we’re testing
  3. How we do things
  4. Who owns what
- Each card must show its last update date.
- The cards should read as entry points for an outsider, not as internal jargon.

Look and feel:
- White page with a lot of air.
- Navy headings.
- Text left-aligned.
- Flat cards with no shadows.
- Buttons are fully round pills.
- Orange primary button with white text.
- Other buttons are outlined in navy.
- States are small round tags, not coloured words.

Scope:
- Keep it to the four cards only.
- Do not add a feed or extra summary blocks.
- Do not introduce new labels or sections beyond the four agreed areas.

Assumption:
- The four area names are the exact front-door labels the room wants.
