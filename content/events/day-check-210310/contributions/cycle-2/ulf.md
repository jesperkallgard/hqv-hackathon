Four cards in one column show the four places an outsider needs: What’s changed, What we’re testing, How we do things, and Who owns what. Each card shows the latest update date and the owner, so staleness is obvious at a glance.

- The front door is only four cards, one for each area.
- The cards sit in one column and each one is a clear place to click next.
- Every card shows its last update date on the face of the card.
- Every card shows who last touched it, so old content is easy to spot.
- The page does not add extra areas, dashboards, or other ways in.

## What it has to do
- Show a single front-door page for the internal site.
- Use exactly four cards, in one vertical column.
- The four card titles are: **What’s changed**, **What we’re testing**, **How we do things**, and **Who owns what**.
- An outsider should understand where to click from the four cards alone.
- Staleness must be obvious before clicking.
- Each card must show the last update date on the card itself.
- Each card must also show who last touched it, on the card itself.

## Content
- Use the four area names exactly as written above.
- Keep the dates visible and easy to scan.
- Use real owner names from the content when available.
- Do not invent new sections or labels.

## Look and feel
- Match the site direction: real internal website, not a document.
- White background, lots of air, navy headings, left-aligned text.
- Flat cards only. No shadows.
- One generous column.
- Buttons, if any appear, must follow the site direction: orange pill for the primary action, outlined navy pills for the rest.

## Out of scope
- No fifth area.
- No dashboards.
- No charts.
- No extra freshness system beyond date and owner on the card.
- No hidden navigation structure.

## Assumption
- The four area names are the exact front-door labels the room wants, so they are used as the only entry points.
